## 2. Novelty assessment (claims A–F)
* **A, B** (networks learn deterministic / pseudorandom generators): known (row 5; kick-world, `15`). Not pursued further.
* **C** (learned model identifies a mechanism from passive data): known in spirit (meta-learned Bayes filters, AB-level); our own results.
* **D** (learned agent selects interventions that reveal hidden structure): **known for closed structural families** (row 1, FT) and as exploration criterion in learned world models (row 2, FT); Bayesian/linear active identification known with declared classes (rows 3–4, FT).
* **E** (learned agent produces and tests structural explanations of a mechanism *outside* its training family): none of the FT sources tests it: row 1 is closed-set; rows 3–4 declare the class; row 5 holds out parameters, not the family. **Not settled by the sources I could read; settled-ness in the AI-scientist literature is unknown (row 9, NA).**
* **F** (agent recognises interface cannot distinguish): our `12`; theory known (Le Cam).
**Selected narrow, potentially unresolved claim (E ∩ D):** *Given a few interventional episodes, can a world model meta-trained on mechanism families F1–F3 estimate structural properties of a mechanism from a structurally held-out family (F4–F5) — by selecting its own interventions — better than generic system identification, direct probing, or a Bayesian design with a declared class; and does it know when it is out of family?* The prior literature predicts negative transfer outside the training family (row 1's "in or near"), and that a strong baseline (direct probing; BED over a class containing the truth) wins when its assumptions hold. A negative result is therefore the expected and informative outcome; **no novelty is claimed in advance.**

## 3. Environment ("mechanism-discovery world", MDW)
Hidden 3-D state with two actuators and two observed channels: x1' = −b1 x1 + g u1; **x2' = −a x2 + c·φ(x1; mechanism) + e u2** (x2 hidden; u2 injects directly into x2, a second admissible intervention); x3' = −b3 x3 + d x2; observations (x1, x3) + 0.03 ε; dt=0.1, T=60 steps/episode, state reset between episodes. The unknown is the coupling φ:
* training families: **F1 linear** φ = x1; **F2 saturating** φ = s·tanh(x1/s); **F3 delayed** φ = x1(t−k).
* structurally held-out families: **H1 gated/hybrid** φ = x1·1[x1>θ]; **H2 backlash/hysteresis** (play operator, width w; memory in an unobserved internal state).
Passive/weak-input data look linear (all mechanisms), so targeted large-amplitude, sign-reversing, timing and cycling interventions are needed. Parameters per instance (s,k,θ,w,c) are random; instances are the replication unit. No metadata/labels/episode lengths differ across families (tested).

## 4. Structural targets (family-agnostic descriptors, computed from the true noiseless simulator under four fixed probe programmes P_small (u1=0.25), P_large (1.0), P_neg (−1.0), P_tri (0→1→0))
nl = 4·R(P_small)/R(P_large) (saturation / gating), asym = −R(P_neg)/R(P_large) (sign asymmetry), lat = first step where x3 > 0.1·R(P_large) under P_large (latency/delay), hyst = x3(T−1 | P_tri)/R(P_large) (cycle residual / memory), with R = x3(T−1). Errors are normalised by the std of each descriptor over the training families (fixed constants). A discovery claim is an estimate of these four numbers (+ an explanation by the fitted simulator), not a label.

## 5. Methods (all get the same budget B ∈ {2,4,8} episodes of 60 steps, 2 actuators)
* **M0 prior** (training-distribution mean) – reference floor.
* **M1 direct probing** – runs the probe programmes themselves in priority order and measures the descriptors from noisy data (unmeasured → prior). Deployable, structured, strong.
* **M2 generic SysID** – ridge NARX (degree-2 polynomial over lagged obs/actions), data from the probe schedule; descriptors by simulating the probes in the fitted model.
* **M3 Bayesian design with declared class** {F1,F2,F3} × coupling gain: grid posterior; next experiment = maximum posterior-weighted predictive variance among candidates; descriptors = posterior mean; goodness-of-fit flag. Knows all linear constants (generous).
* **M4 meta-trained context world model (GRU, 3-seed ensemble)**, trained only on F1–F3 on sequences of 1–8 episodes; descriptors by imagination (probe programmes rolled out autoregressively after the context). Data policies: M4-sweep (probe schedule), M4-random, **M4-plan** (ensemble disagreement over a candidate set, = Plan2Explore criterion on this task).
* **M5 supervised descriptor regressor** (same encoder, direct regression of the four descriptors from context; trained with labels on F1–F3).
* Ablations: no context (pretrained model, empty context) = M0-like; no planner (M4-sweep/random); no uncertainty (ensemble spread/GOF unused).
* **Privileged oracle** (not deployable): the descriptors computed from the true simulator = zero error; the reference for "what budget is needed" is M1 with replicates.

## 6. Outcomes and decision rules (fixed now)
Primary: median normalised descriptor error over the four descriptors, **per family (known F1–F3 pooled, H1, H2 separately)**, per budget, mean over instances with 95 % bootstrap CIs over instances (n=40 per family; paired across methods using identical instances and noise). Epistemic: AUROC of the uncertainty flag (M4 ensemble spread of descriptors; M3 goodness-of-fit) for separating held-out from known family instances and Spearman correlation between flag and error. Efficiency: error vs B. Cost: training and inference seconds.
Rules: (R1) "learned planning helps" iff M4-plan < M4-sweep and < M4-random with non-overlapping CIs at ≥2 budgets on held-out families; (R2) "world model needed" iff the best M4 variant beats both M1 and M2 on held-out families at B=2 or 4 (CIs non-overlapping); (R3) "knows when out of family" iff AUROC ≥ 0.75 for a flag; (R4) if M1 or M2 matches or beats the world model on held-out families, report that the world model is unnecessary for this class of problems.
