# 19 — Structural discovery framework, selected hypotheses and evaluation protocol (written BEFORE the environment code and before any run)

Labels: **[known]** standard result, **[elementary]** short proof, no novelty claimed. Nothing below is claimed new. Literature context: `18`.

## 1. Formal setting
* **Hidden mechanism** M = (S, f, g, ξ): hidden state s ∈ S, input u = (u1,u2) ∈ 𝒰 (admissible programmes: piecewise-continuous, |u_i| ≤ 1, length T, state reset between episodes), dynamics ṡ = f(s,u) + process noise, observation y = g(s) + σε. 𝒮 denotes the world's structural family (here: which coupling construction feeds the hidden variable x2).
* **Interface** I = (observed channels, admissible 𝒰, episode length T, budget B episodes, resets). Interventional response F_M(u) = E[y | do(u)]. **Interventional equivalence** M ≈_I M′ iff F_M(u)=F_M′(u) ∀u ∈ 𝒰 [known].
* **Hypothesis language** 𝓛 = grammar of executable structural hypotheses h = (Π, θ): a finite set Π of primitives drawn from a library 𝒫 (here: linear, quadratic, cubic, tanh(·/s), upper threshold(·−θ), dead-zone(θ), delay(k), play operator(w), hidden self-cubic-damping(κ)) with at most two coupling primitives, plus fitted constants. A hypothesis is **executable**: it yields F_h(u) for every u ∈ 𝒰. 𝓛 is **not** the set of simulator families: it contains primitives and compositions the simulator never instantiates, and **excludes** one simulator family (a resonant hidden second-order mode, "osc"), which is out-of-language by construction.
* **Structural model class** at a given time: 𝓗_t ⊂ 𝓛 (the hypotheses the agent currently entertains). **Misspecification**: M ∉ ≈_I-closure of 𝓗_t. **Discovery success** for a claim ĥ: sup over a fresh admissible test set 𝒰_test of the normalised prediction error of F_ĥ against the noiseless F_M below a fixed tolerance τ (functional recovery; term identity is reported separately because observationally equivalent mechanisms share function).
* **Four uncertainties the agent must separate:** (i) *parameter* (θ within Π: shrinks with data); (ii) *structural* (several Π compatible with the data: ΔBIC small; resolved by discriminating interventions); (iii) *class inadequacy* (every h ∈ 𝓛 fails a calibrated misfit test); (iv) *non-identifiability* (hypotheses that remain pairwise indistinguishable on every admissible programme).

## 2. Propositions (all [known] or [elementary]; each is tested or marked untested)
**S1 [elementary; tested].** Interface-limited exact equivalence. If a threshold coupling x1·1[x1>θ] has θ > sup|x1| = (G/B1)·sup|u1|, it never activates; every such θ yields F ≡ F_null. Hence a "gate" with θ above the reach is not distinguishable from "no coupling" by any admissible programme, and the correct response is certified equivalence rather than a structural claim (negative-control family "unreach").
**S2 [elementary]. Pairwise-disagreement elimination.** In the noiseless case, let 𝓗 be finite and M's response coincide with some h* ∈ 𝓗. Any admissible u with F_h(u) ≠ F_h′(u) falsifies at least one of the two; choosing such a u among the surviving hypotheses removes ≥ 1 wrong hypothesis per experiment, so ≤ |𝓗|−1 experiments leave only h* (up to ≈_I). **Corollary (limit):** if M ∉ 𝓗 the same procedure still removes one of every disagreeing pair but its last survivor is *not* certified: no experiment between members of 𝓗 can reveal that all are wrong. Detecting class inadequacy needs either a generic misfit statistic against the data (used here) or a challenger from a broader class. [textbook model-discrimination logic; consistent with FT row 10 of `18`.]
**S3 [known; Le Cam two-point, Gaussian]. Noisy distinguishability.** For Gaussian observation noise with std σ, two hypotheses whose predicted response to u differs by Δ(u) (vector over the T×channels samples) cannot be told apart with error below ½(1−√(‖Δ‖²/(4σ²))) from that one episode; experiments with ‖Δ(u)‖² ≲ σ² are uninformative about the pair irrespective of the learner. Design score used: ‖Δ‖²/σ̂².
**S4 [known pathology; FT row 10].** EIG-based design inside a declared class that excludes the truth can stall on uninformative designs (Rainforth et al. §5.3). *Instance here:* a class of memoryless u1-couplings never selects a large u2 injection although only that experiment separates saturation from hidden self-damping (tested as an ablation: declared-class BED vs falsification design).
**S5 [untested conjecture, no claim].** For the state-reconstruction step the mapping from (x3,u2) to x2 is mechanism-independent (x2 = ẋ3 + b3·x3 up to scale): a learned estimator trained on any mechanism family transfers if it learns the inversion; so a learned state estimator should *not* beat an analytic reconstruction on held-out mechanisms. The experiment tests this prediction.

## 3. Selected falsifiable hypotheses (all development-only)
Mission candidate ("learned latent + structure search + falsification design beats symbolic regression/system identification alone") is decomposed because `18` shows each ingredient exists separately:
* **H1 (structure generation).** For in-language mechanisms outside a declared 3-family class (quadratic, gate, backlash, hidden feedback), a grammar-guided structure search beats SINDYc (raw, reconstructed or learned state), ridge NARX, a nonparametric static-map (Hammerstein) baseline and declared-class Bayes in functional-recovery rate at B ∈ {5,8}. *Mechanism:* the truth is a short composition in 𝓛 that generic libraries/regressors cannot express (delay, memory, hidden feedback) or approximate poorly from few episodes. *Strongest competitor:* NARX/Hammerstein flexibility. *Fails if:* paired CI of the difference includes 0 at both budgets.
* **H2 (falsification design).** At small budgets (B ∈ {3,5}), choosing the next programme by maximum disagreement among *structurally different* fitted hypotheses beats a fixed sweep, random programmes, ensemble-variance of a generic predictor, and declared-class EIG. *Mechanism:* separating pairs such as sat vs hidden feedback requires a rare programme (large u2). *Competitor:* a well-ordered fixed sweep. *Fails if:* paired CI of difference to the fixed sweep includes 0 at both budgets.
* **H3 (criticism and abstention).** A calibrated misfit test rejects out-of-language mechanisms ("osc") and an equivalence check handles the non-identifiable control ("unreach") without excess false rejection of in-language mechanisms. *Fails if:* osc false-claim rate > 0.20 with criticism, or in-language false-rejection > 0.10, or unreach not recognised (equivalent/null) in < 0.8.
* **H4 (representation role).** A learned state estimator (trained in simulation on lin/sat/delay only, so held-out structure is unseen) improves the *proposal* step (recall@3 of the true structure by equation-error regression) and SINDYc rollout success compared with analytic reconstruction and with raw observations, on held-out families. *Expected:* no (S5). *Fails if:* paired CI includes 0 (then report: representation not needed).
* **H5 (synergy).** The full agent beats each single-component ablation (no criticism, no active design, no structure generation = declared class, no learned state) in *decision accuracy*; synergy is claimed only if every paired CI excludes 0.
Role A (neural imagination as predictor) was tested in `17` (negative, 3 seeds, GRU); it is **not** re-run here (different environment); I state that gap rather than reusing numbers across environments.

## 4. Environment (SDW — structural discovery world)
Hidden x2, observed x1 (actuator-filtered u1), x3 (second hidden-chain readout), **nuisance** x4 (observed independent OU channel, irrelevant), observation noise σ=0.03, hidden process noise on x2 (σ_p=0.02/√s). x1'=−b1x1+g u1; x2' = −a x2 + c·φ(·) + e u2 (+ process noise); x3' = −b3 x3 + d x2. Linear constants a, b3, e, d and the coupling gain c are randomised per instance (±15–30 %). u2 injects directly into x2 (second admissible intervention, also the only way to excite x2 without x1). Truth integrated with 4 substeps per sample (agents' hypotheses use plain Euler at the sampling step, avoiding an exact-simulator "inverse crime"). Episodes T=80, dt=0.1, reset between episodes.
Families (φ): **lin** (x1), **sat** (s·tanh(x1/s)), **quad** (x1²), **delay** (x1(t−k)), **gate** (x1·1[x1>θ]), **backlash** (play operator, width w), **fb** (x1 plus hidden cubic self-damping −κx2³), **osc** (x1 passed through a resonant hidden second-order mode: *outside 𝓛*), **unreach** (gate with θ above the actuator reach: *interventionally equivalent to no coupling*, S1). Declared-class Bayes and the learned estimator only see lin/sat/delay ("known"); quad/gate/backlash/fb are *unfamiliar but in-language*; osc is *out-of-language*; unreach is the *non-identifiable negative control*. No family-specific differences in episode length, action bounds or channel set (tested).

## 5. Agents and baselines (identical observation/intervention interface, equal budget B; episode 1 is the shared low-amplitude u2 pulse; episode 2 is the shared large u1 step; from episode 3 methods choose or follow their schedule)
* **Full agent**: x1 AR model; skeleton (a,b3,k) from the u2-only episode; output-error forward search over primitives (single, then add one) by BIC; model criticism (per-episode χ² against σ̂ estimated from x1 innovations, Bonferroni α=0.01, plus the out-of-sample check on the latest designed episode); falsification-driven design (max standardised disagreement between the best hypothesis and the strongest structurally different alternatives, over a fixed pool of 24 admissible programmes); end-of-budget equivalence test (max disagreement over pool + 200 random admissible programmes below 0.5σ̂ RMSE ⇒ certified equivalent) and abstention (out-of-language when the best hypothesis fails criticism after expansion).
* **Ablations**: no criticism (always claim); no active design (fixed sweep / random / generic-ensemble-disagreement / declared-class EIG); no structure generation (declared class lin/sat/delay only); no learned state; no u2 in the pool.
* **Baselines**: SINDYc via `pysindy 2.1.0` (STLSQ, degree-3 polynomial library, three state sets: raw observed [x1,x3,x4]; delay-embedding/reconstructed [x1,x3,x̂2 from the smoothed derivative]; learned [x1,x3,x̂2 from the learned estimator]); ridge NARX (degree-2, lags); Hammerstein static-map (nonparametric u1→x3 steady-state curve + fitted linear dynamics); declared-class Bayes grid with goodness-of-fit; **PySR/AI-Feynman/weak-/implicit-SINDy are not run** (declared gap: PySR needs Julia; grammar search here is a restricted enumerative stand-in, not a symbolic-regression benchmark).
* **Privilege labels**: no deployable method receives family, parameters or x2. The learned estimator is trained with simulator x2 labels on lin/sat/delay (privileged *training* signal, disclosed); the evaluation oracle (true noiseless F) is used only for scoring.

## 6. Pre-fixed evaluation design
* **Instances** are the replication unit: n=40 per family × 9 families = 360 instances per budget, **paired** (identical instance, noise realisation and shared first two episodes across methods). Budgets B ∈ {3,5,8}.
* **Primary outcome per instance:** functional recovery = RMSE of the claimed hypothesis's predicted x3 against the noiseless true x3 over 24 fresh admissible test programmes ≤ **τ = 0.05** (τ fixed now; ≈ 1.7σ). Secondary: RMSE, term-identity match (primitive set vs the family's expected set), interventions used, runtime.
* **Epistemic outcomes** (full agent and variants with a criticism module): claim/abstain; *claim precision* = P(correct | claim); *coverage* = P(claim); *osc false-claim rate*; *in-language false-rejection rate*; *unreach equivalence/null recognition rate*; AUROC of the misfit statistic for osc vs in-language truth (for every method that can produce one).
* **Decision accuracy** = fraction of instances where the outcome is right: in-language/unreach → a correct claim; osc → abstention (a claim is wrong); false abstention on in-language instances counts as wrong.
* **Statistics:** 95 % percentile bootstrap CIs over instances (10 000 resamples) for each rate and for paired differences; effect sizes reported as risk differences; family-level breakdowns descriptive. Training-seed variance (learned estimator, 3 seeds) is shown separately, not merged into instance CIs.
* **Decision rules** (fixed now): H1 supported iff full-agent-without-criticism minus the best of {SINDYc ×3, NARX, Hammerstein, declared Bayes} has paired CI > 0 on the four unfamiliar in-language families at B=5 **and** 8. H2 supported iff falsification design minus each of {fixed sweep, random, generic-ensemble, declared-class EIG} has CI > 0 at ≥ 2 budgets among {3,5,8} (the fixed-sweep comparison is decisive). H3 as stated above. H4 iff learned-estimator variants exceed analytic reconstruction (CI > 0) on held-out families. H5 as stated. A hypothesis that fails its rule is reported as not supported; no re-tuning on the evaluation namespace.
* **Namespaces (new, unexposed):** estimator training 24100+, one **debug-only smoke** (functionality, no tuning of thresholds) 24500, evaluation instances 25000+, noise 25000+, test programmes 26000+. Reserved 600–629/700–719/800–829 untouched; earlier namespaces not reused. No confirmatory run; the evaluation set is a development look and is "exposed" afterwards.
* **Tuned constants** (fixed before any run, from theory not data): α=0.01 Bonferroni, ΔBIC window 10, equivalence ε=0.5σ̂, τ=0.05, grids listed in the code. SINDYc threshold and the NARX ridge λ are the only baseline hyper-parameters tuned, on the *known* families at a separate namespace (24200+) before evaluation.

## 7. Failure conditions of the whole programme
If the grammar agent does not beat NARX/Hammerstein on the unfamiliar in-language families (H1 fails) the structural-generation claim is unsupported at this scale; if design does not beat a fixed sweep (H2) the active-selection claim reverts to "known, no advantage here"; if criticism cannot reject osc without rejecting in-language truth (H3), model criticism by residual test is insufficient in this problem; if the learned estimator equals analytic reconstruction (H4) the representation role is unnecessary here (as S5 predicts).

## 8. Addendum A (written after the debug smoke at ns 24500 and the baseline tuning at ns 24200, **before the evaluation namespace 25000 was run**)
* **What the debug smoke showed (1 instance per family, functionality only; not used to change any threshold, rule, α, ΔBIC window, ε or τ):** the pipeline runs end to end; every SINDYc configuration with a polynomial library of degree ≥ 2 diverged in free-run rollout (clipped at ±8).  Implementation fixes made *before* looking at any evaluation data: (i) the hypothesis simulator uses the same 4-substep zero-order-hold integration as the truth (plain Euler at the sample step produced a systematic misfit that the structure search absorbed into spurious second terms); (ii) primitives are evaluated at interval midpoints (delay at sample end); (iii) the actuator AR model is estimated by instrumental variables (the least-squares estimate was biased by measurement noise in the regressor); (iv) continuous primitive parameters are refined by least squares and the gate-threshold grid is finer (0.05 steps).
* **Baseline tuning at ns 24200 on KNOWN families only (30 instances, B=5, sweep-B):** NARX ridge λ = 1.0; SINDYc library degree 1 (linear) with STLSQ threshold 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.05 / 0.3 for the state sets raw / embed / learned / oracle — i.e. *the tuning criterion on near-linear known families selects a linear library*; the nonlinear-library alternatives diverged in rollout (median RMSE 1.0–4.0).  To avoid reporting only a linear SINDYc as "the" SINDYc, a second configuration **SINDYc-dict** (linear terms + per-variable x², x³, tanh(x/0.5), relu(x); no cross terms) was added with thresholds 1.0 / – / 0.3 / 0.1 (raw / – / learned / oracle) tuned the same way.  Both are reported; neither is PySR, weak-SINDy or implicit-SINDy.
* **Baseline data schedule:** baselines and the grammar-without-design reference use sweep B (interleaved u1/u2, the information-richer fixed order); sweep A (u1 first) is run for the grammar agent and for the declared-class/NARX/Hammerstein baselines as the secondary schedule.  The decisive "fixed sweep" comparison for H2 is against the *better* of the two fixed sweeps per budget (reported, not hidden).
* **Outcome variants derived post hoc from the same stored statistics (no new runs):** `full` = in-sample χ² + out-of-sample + ambiguity (protocol §5); `crit_in` = in-sample only + ambiguity; `nocrit` = always claim.  An ε sensitivity table (ε ∈ {0.25,0.5,1,2}σ̂) is **analysis only**; the primary ε remains 0.5σ̂.

## 9. Addendum B (written after the primary development look at ns 25000, **before** the calibration/test follow-up runs at ns 27000 / 28000)
* **What the primary look showed that motivates a follow-up (details in `20`):** the in-sample χ² criticism ranks out-of-language instances well (AUROC 0.98 at B=8) but is **not null-calibrated**: false rejection of valid in-language claims was 0.26 (full) / 0.15 (in-sample only) at B=8 against a nominal ≤ 0.01 — H3's rule (false rejection ≤ 0.10) **failed** as pre-stated.  The primary results are final and are not re-scored.  The question for the follow-up is whether this is a *calibration artefact* (fixable by an empirical null) or inherent.
* **Correction to the analysis script made before reading the H1 verdict:** H1 is evaluated against the baselines the protocol lists (SINDYc variants, NARX, Hammerstein, declared-class Bayes); the script had also allowed the agent's own `grammar_sweep` (the agent without design) to count as a "baseline"; it is an ablation and is excluded from the H1 comparison (still reported in the tables).
* **Follow-up design (pre-fixed):** (i) *calibration run* ns 27000: 20 instances for each KNOWN family (lin, sat, delay; 60 instances), agents `falsify` and `sweepB`, B ∈ {3,5,8}; the calibrated criticism threshold for budget B is the ⌈(n+1)(1−0.05)⌉/n empirical quantile of the in-sample misfit statistic −log10 p_in over those 60 *valid* fits (target false-rejection 5 %); (ii) *test run* ns 28000: 30 instances per family × 9 families (270), same agents and budgets; the calibrated rule rejects iff −log10 p_in > threshold(B); ambiguity rule unchanged (ε=0.5σ̂).  Outcomes: in-language false-rejection by group (known / unfamiliar), osc claim rate, unreach recognition, claim precision and coverage, decision accuracy; paired against the uncalibrated rule on the same instances.  Success rule for "calibration artefact": false rejection on the *unfamiliar* in-language families ≤ 0.10 **and** osc claim rate ≤ 0.20 **and** decision accuracy of the calibrated full agent ≥ that of the no-criticism agent at B=8 (CIs over instances).  Calibrating on known families only deliberately tests *transfer* of the null to unfamiliar valid structures.
* **Post-hoc addition (labelled as such):** the primary look found the learned state estimator > naive derivative reconstruction (analytic) for proposal recall, with oracle = learned.  Because "analytic" there was a Savitzky–Golay derivative (a weak analytic competitor), a model-based **Kalman/RTS-smoother reconstruction** (skeleton known from the u2-only episode, process noise absorbing the unknown coupling) is added as a third reconstruction and evaluated on the *same* 360 instances (observations regenerated from the stored seeds).  It was not pre-registered; it is reported as post hoc.
