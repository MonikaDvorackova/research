# Candidate theorems and counterexamples — research log (embedded observers)

Labels: **P** proved (complete proof in the cited file), **K** known (source), **E** elementary, **C** conjecture, **O** computational observation (exact finite computation, not a proof), **U** unsupported hypothesis. Novelty: **L** low, **M** medium, **U** uncertain. Each entry follows the ten-step method of the directive: question, model, target, assumptions, conjecture, closest theorem, proof attempt, disproof attempt, comparison, outcome. Verdicts per direction at the end. Model background: `embedded-observer-formal-model.md`.

## Entry E1 — embedding reduces to a finite-state controller of a POMDP
*Question.* Does physical embedding (observer as a subsystem of a finite host) change the identifiability theory relative to an external POMDP controller? *Model.* Finite host H = (S_E × S_O, T, μ₀), interface (α, ω, r = o). *Target.* Law of the observer-visible process. *Conjecture.* If the host is sequentially factored (independent noise for environment and observer updates, product initial law), the visible process equals that of a POMDP under a finite-state controller. *Closest theorem.* Finite-state-controller POMDP planning (standard; not accessed) and the identification of an agent with its policy (elementary). *Proof.* Theorem 1 (**P**, formal-model §2); exact check **O** (agreement 3.3×10⁻¹⁶). *Disproof attempt.* Fails only when SF1 fails (E2). *Outcome.* True; **elementary and known in substance** (novelty L). Consequence: no embedding-specific identification theorem exists inside the SF class; the only embedding content there is the finite, time-homogeneous memory restriction (E5).

## Entry E2 — shared noise: embedding as confounded or instrumented intervention
*Conjecture.* Without SF1 there are hosts with identical observer-visible laws and different interventional laws. *Proof.* Theorem 2, explicit two-host construction (**P**), verified exactly (**O**). *Closest theorem.* Causal Hierarchy Theorem (Bareinboim–Correa–Ibeling–Icard, R-60, Thm 1; accessed), instrumental-variable identification (Balke–Pearl, Imbens–Angrist; not accessed), Bell-type measurement-dependence relaxations (Barrett–Gisin 2011: all singlet correlations reproducible with ≤ 1 bit of mutual information between a setting and the hidden variable; accessed). *Outcome.* **Known (K)**; novelty L. *Residual question.* A quantitative bound of the bias in terms of the mutual information I(A;U): see E8.

## Entry E3 — memory-capacity (label) bound
*Conjecture.* An observer whose conclusion is a function of its final memory state can reliably (error < ½ for each) separate at most m behaviourally distinct hypotheses; a K-way identification needs m ≥ K. *Proof (E).* The conclusion is g(M_T) with g : S_O → labels, so at most m labels; correct classification with error < ½ of K hypotheses needs K distinct labels. *Outcome.* True, elementary; relates to Fano; novelty L. Not embedding-specific (applies to any finite-state learner).

## Entry E4 — strict memory hierarchy
*Conjecture.* For every m there are mechanisms indistinguishable by every m-state controller but distinguishable by an (m+1)-state controller. *Proof (E).* A "combination lock" revealing its difference only after a specific action sequence of length m+1 requires a counter of ≥ m+2 states for a deterministic controller; with randomisation the success probability decays exponentially in m. *Outcome.* True, elementary; novelty L.

## Entry E5 — time-homogeneity floor (the genuine embedding restriction) and its extension
*Known (K).* Hellman–Cover (1970) (restated in B. T. K. Leung, arXiv:2009.11917, accessed; original not accessed): with an m-state time-invariant automaton and i.i.d. signals of likelihood-ratio bound λ, the optimal asymptotic error between two simple hypotheses is 1/(1+λ^{m−1}) (for symmetric priors; infimum approached, not attained); Cover (1969) shows time-varying automata can reach error 0. A recurrent consequence: an embedded finite-state observer cannot identify a noisy mechanism with vanishing error. *O.* My exact search reproduces 1/3 (m = 2) and 0.2009 (m = 3) at λ = 2, K = 1.
*New question.* What if the informative event occurs only in a hidden periodic phase (period K), with no clock? *Computation (O).* Optimal m = 2 error equals ½ − c/K in 24 (p, K) cells to 10⁻¹⁵ (`results/embedded/m2_exact_check.json`). *Conjecture → theorem.* **Theorem 3 (P)**, `proof-note-two-state-hidden-phase.md`: for m = 2 the optimum is attained by the memoryless last-signal automaton and persistence cannot be exploited. *Disproof attempt.* Global search over the 4-parameter family found no violation; the sufficiency proof covers all K and all c. *Closest theorem.* Hellman–Cover (the K = 1 case). *Outcome.* **Proved but modest; novelty U (probably L): a special-case calculation in a classical framework.** *Open:* general m. Stochastic searches suggest m = 3 beats the "random-phase dilution" value slightly (0.334 vs 0.3378 at K = 2) — unreliable (non-convex, boundary optima), **U**, no claim.

## Entry E6 — self-reference: self-knowledge barrier
*Conjecture.* An observer that is part of the mechanism faces an identification barrier from self-reference beyond behavioural equivalence and resource bounds. *Attempt.* (i) The observer's own kernel κ on unvisited (memory, observation) pairs is not identified by its trajectory (reachability; **E**). (ii) An m-state observer cannot store its complete own transition table (|table| ≈ m·|Y|·log m bits > log m): finite-state quine impossibility (**E**). (iii) For infinite-class settings, diagonal constructions: Wolpert (2008) Thm 1 (no two distinguishable inference devices can weakly infer each other) and Thm 3 (no two devices can strongly infer each other) hold independent of physical laws but require a device model with *arbitrary setup functions* (stronger than our finite embedded model, where setup is determined by the host dynamics); the grain-of-truth literature (Leike–Taylor–Fallenstein 2016, reflective oracles; Demski–Garrabrant 2019) addresses realizability failure for computable agents embedded in computable environments and gives a positive construction. *Disproof attempt.* I could not formulate a finite-state self-reference limitation not implied by (i)–(ii) plus the memory bound E3. *Outcome.* **No new barrier; reduces to behavioural equivalence + resource bounds + known inference-device theory.** Novelty L.

## Entry E7 — endogenous interventions as instruments
*Conjecture.* If the observer's realised action is a = g(z, U) with U a hidden variable shared with the environment, identifiable interventional content equals what instrumental analysis identifies. *Attempt.* Immediate from the structure (z instrument, U unobserved confounder): the intent-to-treat law is identified, effects of a only partially (Balke–Pearl bounds; not accessed). *Outcome.* **K/E**, novelty L. Direction B (endogenous interventions) reduces to E1–E2 and IV theory.

## Entry E8 — measurement-dependence sensitivity bound
*Conjecture.* If the observer's randomisation is dependent on the hidden environment variable with mutual information ι, the bias of the observational estimate of the interventional law is at most √(ι/2) in total variation (per action, averaged over actions in the mean-square sense). *Proof (P, elementary).* P(Y|a) − P(Y|do(a)) = Σ_u [P(u|a) − P(u)] P(Y|a,u) ⇒ TV ≤ TV(P(U|a), P(U)) ≤ √(½ KL(P(U|a)‖P(U))) (Pinsker); averaging over a with weights P(a): E_a[TV²] ≤ ι/2 (Jensen) where ι = I(A;U). *Closest.* Rosenbaum Γ and marginal sensitivity models, E-values (VanderWeele–Ding), Bell relaxations (Barrett–Gisin, Hall; accessed first). *Outcome.* **Elementary, known in form**; novelty L. It quantifies, but does not change, E2.

## Entry E9 — counterfactual access without external replay (persistent latents)
*Question.* Can an embedded observer infer cross-world functionals from within-trajectory data? *Model.* Persistent latent U (static) with fresh noise ε_t per step; Y_t = f(a_t, U, ε_t). *Attempt.* The observed law is a finite mixture over U of conditionally independent products (a latent-class model whose "views" are the repeated measurements under chosen actions). By Kruskal's theorem (Allman–Matias–Rhodes 2009, Thm 1/Thm 4 with p ≥ 3 views; accessed) the mixing law and the conditional kernels p(y|a,u) are generically identifiable up to label permutation; hence functionals of the *U-coupled, ε-independent* cross-time joint (e.g. type-level treatment-effect heterogeneity) are identified. The *same-ε* cross-world coupling is not identified: counterexample as in the Result A of `technical-investigation-counterfactual-coupling.md` with U fresh each step. *Outcome.* **K** (latent-class identification; panel/N-of-1 designs in causal inference — not accessed) plus E; novelty L. The exact boundary between identified and unidentified couplings is "measurable w.r.t. the persistent σ-algebra", elementary. Direction D reduces.

## Entry E10 — observer–host resource coupling
*Conjecture.* If the observer's compute is allocated from the host, there are statistical–computational separations beyond cryptographic indistinguishability. *Attempt.* If the host simulates the observer, the observer's time is at most polynomial in the host's; any computational-indistinguishability statement for polynomial-time distinguishers transfers with a polynomial overhead. A host of size N can hide a pseudorandom source from all embedded observers of size ≤ N^c iff a PRG fooling size-N^c circuits exists (non-constructively with seed O(log N) by counting; constructively under hardness assumptions: Nisan–Wigderson/Impagliazzo–Wigderson, not accessed). *Disproof attempt.* None found for a distinct separation. *Outcome.* **Reduces to known derandomisation/PRG theory** (K, not accessed; PRF facts via Aaronson §7.1, accessed). Novelty L.

## Entry E11 — can learned world models give a provable advantage under bounded computation?
*Attempt.* Information-theoretic identifiability is independent of the learner (formal-model §8); a provable advantage would have to be statistical/computational (e.g. approximation of effective laws by neural classes): standard learning theory, no embedding-specific content found. *Outcome.* No distinct mathematical contribution; STOP.
