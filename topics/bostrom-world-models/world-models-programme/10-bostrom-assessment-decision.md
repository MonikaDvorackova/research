# 10 — Bostrom connection, critical assessment, decision

## Bostrom (updated)
Bostrom's trilemma (Phil. Quarterly 53(211), 2003) is a statement about the distribution of observers, not about observable signatures; nothing here tests its third proposition. The experiments probe a different, narrower question: **which classes of generative mechanism are epistemically accessible to an embedded agent through a restricted interface**, and what a learned world model changes about that.
The five items are different claims and the study touches only the first two, inside toy generators: (i) detecting a numerical approximation (quantisation vs stiction: possible with reachable thresholds, exactly impossible beyond actuator reach); (ii) a resource-bounded generator (delay/reset vs ordinary lag: possible with sufficiently informative interventions); (iii) a computational implementation, (iv) "this environment is simulated", (v) "our universe is an ancestor simulation" — **not addressed**. A detected quantisation signature supports at most an interface-relative mechanism-class claim (level 3–4), never level 5, without further premises.
Candidate central contribution, stated as a hypothesis not a result: *learned world models alter the practical (sample, compute, robustness) limits of discovery but not the interface-relative identifiability limits (P1, P5 in `08`)*. The experiments are consistent with this (probes reach the matched-oracle level with good interventions; every probe fails to abstain on exactly equivalent worlds unless trained on ambiguity, and abstention only partly transfers), but they do not prove it and the identifiability half is a known two-point-testing fact.

## Critical assessment
* The benchmark is a family of 1-D first-order systems with deterministic latent dynamics; the oracle shares the generator's structure; the learned models see the same hypothesis space in training and test (except parameter/noise/family holdouts). "Discovery" here is classification among six known classes — not discovery of unknown structure.
* The strongest simple competitors are strong: an untrained recurrent net already gives 0.66 (sweep), a hand-designed amplitude sweep matches learned planning outcomes and beats the learned planner, and an end-to-end supervised GRU is not better than probes on world-model states.
* The one pre-registered-in-plan treatment (ICI) failed its primary outcome; a robustness-to-noise advantage appeared post hoc in 10/10 cells and a small RSSM-only ID advantage (no paired test). Both need confirmation before being believed.
* Statistical level: 3 training seeds per cell; instances as test unit; no multiplicity correction; single seed for transfer.
* Known results explain most findings: experimental design (EIG), meta-learning as Bayes filtering (Ortega 2019; Mikulik 2020, AB), non-identifiability (Le Cam), covariate shift. **No novelty claim.** The only candidate original observations are (a) the ICI noise-robustness pattern (unconfirmed, post hoc) and (b) the partial transfer of abstention to an unseen equivalence family (single design, effect sizes modest) — both unverified against the literature.

## Decision: **REVISE**
The central hypothesis ("a learned latent world model can discover hidden structure and recognise unidentifiability") survives only in a weak form: structure is linearly readable at near-oracle level under good interventions, but recognising unidentifiability does not emerge from prediction-trained models and only partially from exposure. NO-GO is not warranted (clean negative controls and controlled comparisons exist); GO is not warranted (no robust learned-specific advantage).

## Strongest next experiment (a development-stage one)
Test whether **uncertainty about identifiability** can be learned in a way that transfers: train world-model ensembles with calibrated (conformal/posterior-predictive) class sets on identifiable data only, evaluate set size on eqA/eqB/unseen equivalence families; compare with the exposure-trained probe and with the oracle's posterior; add paired instance-level tests and ≥5 seeds, and a confirmatory-grade replication of the ICI noise-robustness pattern with ICI variants that keep σ shared vs varied within a view pair.

Single most important question for the next milestone: **can a prediction-trained latent model *know that it cannot know* — i.e. produce calibrated set-valued claims that include both members of an equivalent pair on mechanisms it has never seen paired — without being shown ambiguous examples?**
