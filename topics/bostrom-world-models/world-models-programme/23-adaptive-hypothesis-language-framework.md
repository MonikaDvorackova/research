# 23 — Adaptive hypothesis-language framework and pre-registered protocol (Phase 22)

Status: **written before the calibration, development and holdout runs** (committed first; see git history). Internal document; development evidence only; no confirmatory run is authorised. Doc 22 records the literature and baselines, doc 24 the experiments.

## 1. Question and scope
Can an agent (i) detect, with a calibrated test, that its initial hypothesis language L0 cannot explain what it observes, (ii) construct an executable, complexity-penalised structural extension, (iii) choose interventions that can falsify it, and (iv) abstain when the mechanism is outside every language it can build or is not identifiable through the intervention interface?
Doc 20 showed that structure search works when the right primitives are *already in the grammar*. This study removes that alignment: the held-out mechanisms use operators, state dependencies and compositions that are **not in L0**. It does **not** claim open-ended discovery: the agent's extensions are drawn from a larger, staged, designer-specified closure (§3), so the achievable claim is *criticism-triggered, minimal-sufficient expansion within a stated closure*, and the study measures where that closure stops working (the `saw` family is outside it by construction).

## 2. Formal setting
* H₀ = L0: the hypothesis class of executable simulators `x2' = −a x2 − κ x2³ + k u2 + Σ_j w_j τ_j(x1)`, `x3' = −b3 x3 + x2` with τ_j in the stage-0 term set (doc 19 grammar). H₁ ⊂ H₂ ⊂ … are the cumulative expansions of §3. I = admissible interface = programmes (u1(t), u2(t)), |u| ≤ 1, T = 80 samples, observation (x1, x3, x4) with noise σ = 0.03. D_t = episodes collected by time t; π = the experimental policy.
* **Expressiveness ≠ identifiability.** For a true mechanism m, define the interventional map F_m : I → R^T. (a) *L0-misspecified*: inf_{h∈H₀} sup_{u∈I} ‖F_m(u) − F_h(u)‖ > 0. (b) *Extension-recoverable*: ∃ h ∈ H₁ with F_h = F_m on I. (c) *Distinguishable*: the Le Cam two-point bound ½(1 − TV) with the Gaussian noncentrality δ² = ‖F_m(u) − F_h(u)‖²/σ² is small for some admissible u (S3 of doc 19), so a finite experiment separates m from the best h₀ ∈ H₀. (d) *Non-identifiable even after expansion*: F_m = F_{m′} on I for a structurally different m′ (doc 19 S1: a threshold above the actuator reach, `thresh`, and a bump centred outside the reach, `bumpfar`).
* **Proposition P1 (elementary, no novelty claimed).** If m is L0-misspecified with gap ε = inf_{h∈H₀} max_{u∈D}‖F_m(u)−F_h(u)‖/σ over the *collected* programmes D, the likelihood-ratio test of H₀ against the saturated alternative rejects with power → 1 as ε → ∞ at fixed level; a finite number of episodes therefore suffices to reject all of H₀ **only if some collected programme has ε ≫ 1** — which depends on the policy (a programme that does not excite the missing structure, e.g. a u2-only programme for a coupling that needs u1, gives ε = 0).
* **Proposition P2 (counterexample class, elementary).** Expansion does not guarantee discovery: if m ∈ H₁ but F_m restricted to the collected programmes equals F_h for some h ∈ H₀ ∪ H₁ with a different structure (e.g. `relu(x²−θ)` and `abs(x)+x²` agree on a narrow excitation range), the data cannot choose between them, and criticism cannot reject either. Predictive equivalence on D is therefore never evidence of structural equivalence; this is measured in doc 24 (§3 levels).
* **Proposition P3 (known, used as design score).** For finite rival sets, an experiment u separates h from h′ at noise level σ when ‖F_h(u)−F_h′(u)‖/σ is large (Le Cam; Gaussian noncentrality). The design score is the maximum such disagreement between the leading hypothesis and its structurally different rivals. When the truth lies outside the rival set the survivor is not certified (doc 19 S2/S4; Rainforth et al. 2024 §5.3).
Nothing here is claimed as a new theorem; the checks against the literature are in doc 22.

## 3. Hypothesis language, expansion and criticism (as implemented in `src/eid/ool/lang.py`, `loop.py`)
Term = outer(inner). Stage 0 (L0): id, sq, cube, tanh(p), gate(θ), dz(θ), play(w) on inner id; id(delay k); hidden-state feedback κ x2³. Stage 1 (new static operators): abs, sqrtabs, sin(q), bump(p), relu(θ). Stage 2 (new state variables): id(lp τ) (leaky integral of x1), id(rate) (actuator rate). Stage 3 (new compositions): inner ∈ {sq, delay k, lp τ} with any non-trivial outer. Stage 4 (new state–state interaction): γ·x1·x2 (hidden-state-dependent gain). Every term outside stage 0 is *new vocabulary* and is charged `EXP_PEN = 1` parameter-equivalents in BIC on top of its 1–3 parameters. Parameters: grids + least-squares polish; skeleton (a, b3, k) from the u2-only episode, re-estimated jointly (two passes). **Honest limit:** the stage vocabularies are chosen by the designer; the staged search is a criticism-triggered, ordered traversal of a closure, not synthesis from nothing. The benchmark mechanisms (`manifest.json`, §4) are authored separately and the agent never reads it.
**Loop per revision (executed by the code, trace stored):** (1) *Predict*: fit the best L0 hypothesis. (2) *Criticise*: per-episode χ² against σ̂ (IV-AR innovations of x1); statistic s_in = −log₁₀ min p; reject when s_in > c_in(B), the **empirical 95 % quantile over valid fits** on the calibration namespace (§5); out-of-sample statistic s_oos on each newly designed episode, threshold c_oos(B) likewise. (3) *Diagnose*: on rejection the agent evaluates extension classes one at a time (L0 ∪ stage s) and records for each the best hypothesis, ΔBIC against the best L0 hypothesis and its s_in — the class whose best hypothesis is accepted identifies the *type* of inadequacy (missing operator / state / composition / interaction / insufficient evidence). (4) *Generate*: the winning stage's best hypothesis, an explicit executable object with a label and parameters. (5) *Discriminate*: the next programme maximises the disagreement between the leading hypothesis and up to three structurally different rivals (`falsify`). (6) *Test*: the programme is run and the *previous* leading hypothesis is scored out-of-sample on it. (7) *Revise*: refit; accept when ΔBIC(L0 − new) > 10 **and** s_in ≤ c_in(B). (8) *Stop/abstain*: `claim` (accepted, unambiguous), `abstain_amb` (rival within ΔBIC < 10 whose predictions differ by > ε = 0.5 σ̂ on 124 programmes), `abstain_ool` (nothing passes criticism after the five stage tests and the combined search; the best-BIC hypothesis is then reported as a *predictive surrogate only*). No black-box function is ever reported as a discovery.

## 4. Benchmark (independent, frozen)
`src/eid/ool/manifest.json` (sha256 recorded in the result files) declares each mechanism as an expression of x1 (`x`), its rate (`xd`), a leaky integral (`z`), a delayed value (`xdel`) and the hidden state (`x2`), with parameter ranges and the structural signature the evaluator will accept. Dynamics are those of the SDW (same actuator, hidden x2, readout x3, nuisance x4, noise 0.03/0.02), so the old learned estimator transfers without retraining. `bench.World` exposes only `run(U)`; the agent cannot read specs, labels or parameters. Splits: **calib** (L0-expressible, for criticism calibration): lin, sat, quad, gate, delay. **dev** (tuned on): lin (control), sin [A], rectsq [C], leak [B], bilin [C], thresh [N]. **holdout** (frozen before any agent run; evaluated once): bump [A], sqrtabs [A], rate [B], gdelay [C], quadlag [C], bumpfar [N], saw [beyond the closure]. Every identifiable mechanism has max-programme effect ≥ 30 σ over the no-coupling null (`results/ool_dev/detectability.json`); `thresh`/`bumpfar` are numerically null. No renaming of L0 operators is used; `sin`, `bump`, `sqrtabs`, `relu∘sq`, `lp`, `rate`, `gate∘delay`, `sq∘lp`, `x1·x2` are absent from L0. Caveat: the author of the benchmark also wrote the stage-1…4 vocabularies; the vocabularies were fixed from the three qualitative forms requested (A operator, B state, C composition) *before* looking at any agent result, and `saw` and the two non-identifiable families are the independent stress points.

## 5. Calibration, development, holdout — separation and namespaces
Tuning (baselines only): instance seeds 310–312, calib+dev families. **Calibration** namespace 29000: calib families × 12 instances, L0 agent with falsify design (no expansion), B ∈ {5, 8, 12}; c_in(B), c_oos(B) = 95 % quantiles of the statistics over these valid fits (false rejection on correct models ≈ 5 % by construction, verified on dev controls). **Development** namespace 30000: dev families × 10. **Holdout** namespace 31000: holdout families × 10, run **once** after the agent, thresholds and baselines are frozen; its results are still development-stage evidence because the author wrote both the agent and the benchmark, and the namespace is consumed. Reserved ranges 600–629, 700–719, 800–829 are untouched. Test programmes: 24 fresh random programmes per instance (seed 1 000 000 + index), noise-free truth.

## 6. Conditions and baselines (matched data)
All methods in the primary comparison see the **same episodes**: E0 (u2 square), E1 (u1 = +1), then the SWEEP_B schedule, B ∈ {5, 8, 12} total episodes. Hidden-state-reconstruction front ends: tuned RTS–Kalman (skeleton from E0, process noise by innovation likelihood), the learned biGRU of doc 20 (3 seeds, trained on SDW known families only), and a privileged oracle (reported as an upper bound).
Agent family: `adaptive` (full loop), `L0` (fixed-language structural search), `static_all` (all stage-0…4 vocabularies searched at once, no criticism gating — the broad static grammar), `oracle_structure` (privileged: fits the true signature), ordering variants `kalman_order`/`learned_order` (stage order decided by equation-error evidence on a reconstructed state; role of the world model), designs `falsify` / `sweepB` / `random` / `ens` (NARX-ensemble variance, neural-intervention-prediction role).
Equation-discovery / system-identification baselines: **PySR 2.7.2** with a strong fixed library (+ − ×; square, cube, tanh) and an expanded library (+ − × / max; square, cube, tanh, abs, sin, exp, sign, sqrt∘abs), run on the coupling drive recovered from a reconstructed hidden state with a tapped delay line (x1 lags 1, 3–9) as inputs; PySR on raw observations with no latent (role A); **SINDYc**, **weak-form SINDy**, **implicit (SINDy-PI-style) SINDy** (pysindy 2.1.0 / own implementation, configuration chosen on the tuning namespace); ridge NARX; Hammerstein static map; direct probing is covered by the Hammerstein/NARX probes. Compute (wall clock, candidates) is logged for every method.

## 7. Outcome metrics (fixed)
For every instance and every method's final model: **L1 predictive equivalence** RMSE ≤ τ = 0.05 on the 24 fresh programmes; **L2 structural equivalence** — the discovered term set equals the signature in the manifest (for equation-discovery baselines: a documented token-signature check, only for the families where it is definable; otherwise "not scored"); **L3 mechanistic identification** — L2 and the key parameter within tolerance (θ ± 0.07, q ± 20 %, τ ± 25 %, k ± 1, γ ± 35 %, bump p ± 25 %). For non-identifiable families the correct answer is the null explanation (or abstain_amb); for `saw` the correct answer is abstain_ool. **False discovery** = claim whose structure ≠ signature. **Abstention accuracy** on non-identifiable / beyond-closure families. Cost: observations (episodes, steps), interventions, candidate columns evaluated, stage tests, wall-clock.
Statistics: the instance is the replication unit; 95 % percentile bootstrap (10 000 resamples) over instances within pooled strata; paired differences on identical instances; no multiplicity correction beyond reporting every comparison (the confirmatory-style claims below are the only decision rules).

## 8. Hypotheses and decision rules (fixed in advance)
* **H1 (primary).** On the *identifiable out-of-L0* mechanisms (dev: sin, rectsq, leak, bilin; holdout: bump, sqrtabs, rate, gdelay, quadlag) at B = 8 and 12, the adaptive agent's L2 rate exceeds the best of {PySR-fixed, PySR-expanded, weak-SINDy, implicit-SINDy, SINDYc, L0-search} by a paired difference whose 95 % CI lower bound is > 0, **and** its L1 rate is within 0.05 of the best baseline's. If `static_all` matches the adaptive agent (L2 difference CI ⊇ [−0.05, 0.05]) the claim is downgraded from "adaptive expansion improves recovery" to "expansion is an efficiency device", with efficiency judged by candidates evaluated and wall-clock.
* **H2 (secondary, world model).** Replacing the classical reconstruction with the learned latent, or ordering the expansion stages by learned-latent equation-error evidence, increases L2 (or lowers candidates evaluated at equal L2) with paired CI lower bound > 0. Otherwise the learned world model is removed from the recommended architecture.
* **H3 (tertiary, falsification).** `falsify` reaches a correct claim (L2, claim) at a smaller budget than `sweepB` and `random`: the mean over instances of the first B ∈ {5, 8, 12} with a correct claim (12 + 4 if never) is lower with paired CI upper bound < 0.
* **H4 (criticism).** The calibrated test has false rejection ≤ 0.10 on valid dev controls at each B, and power (rejection of L0 on out-of-L0 families) ≥ 0.8 at B = 12. High AUROC alone is not evidence.
Any result not meeting its rule is reported as not supported. A failure of H1 against `static_all` or PySR-expanded is a valid and expected-informative outcome.

## 9. Falsification plan (after development results, development families only)
Noise ×2 and ×3; reduced budgets (B = 5); intervention pool without u2; α-mis-set criticism threshold (c_in halved and doubled); hidden-state reconstruction error injected into the front ends (additive noise on x̂2); strongly correlated rivals (`rectsq` vs abs+sq: ambiguity rate); stiff dynamics (a, b3 × 4); increased expression complexity (two stacked mechanisms is **not** implemented — listed as untested). The failure boundary and every omitted difficulty are reported in doc 24.

## 10. Honesty constraints
Staged vocabularies are designer-specified; PySR/SINDy use my configurations (tuned on a separate namespace; the best configuration of each is reported along with the full tuning table); the learned estimator uses simulator hidden-state labels as a training signal (disclosed); the benchmark and the agent share an author; no peer review exists; nothing is claimed about the simulation hypothesis.
