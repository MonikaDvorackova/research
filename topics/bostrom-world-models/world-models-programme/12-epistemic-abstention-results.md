# 12 — Can a learned world model tell when a mechanism cannot be identified? Development results

Plan and rules: `11-…` (written before any evaluation on namespaces ≥ 11000). Code: `src/eid/gwb/{epi,epi_agents}.py`, `scripts/epi_*`. Raw: `results/epi_dev/`, `results/epi_final/`. Scope: development only; world models = frozen `det_pred_s{1,2,3}` (prediction-only) for probes; conditional-simulator bank trained for this study on three new seeds (701–703 namespace) on identifiable data only; test namespace 11200 (fresh), calibration 11100/11110, audit 12000, budget curves 13000. Not confirmatory. Caveats: seeds=3; all categories are toy 1-D generators; instances are the unit (Wilson 95 % CIs shown); the oracle shares the generator's structure.

## 1. Protocol
Every method gets 48 steps. Adaptive methods (M1 oracle EIG, M7 learned simulators) start with a common passive block and choose 5 further 8-step blocks; probes (M2–M6) read a fixed designed sweep. Claim threshold per method is set on 1000 identifiable calibration instances to give **70 % claim coverage**, then applied unchanged to every category. Equivalent worlds (eqA–eqD) are exact (unit-tested: identical observation laws); `approx`, `idnoisy` are identifiable in principle; `ooc_*` lie outside the six-class hypothesis class.

## 3. Hypotheses
* **H1 supported (this benchmark).** Standard confidence is not informative about non-identifiability: probes claim on 94–96 % of eqC and 80–83 % of eqD at 70 % coverage; temperature scaling changes nothing; conformal singletons remain ≥0.93 on eqC; an ensemble helps only for one out-of-class mechanism (ooc_osc 0.09 vs 0.53–0.92) and not for equivalent worlds. Under σ×3 noise shift (`idnoisy`) the identifiable-calibrated softmax probes (M2/M3/M6) make wrong claims on 48–60 % of instances (accuracy among claimed 0.24–0.28; the exposure probe M5 and learned simulators M7 are no better: 38–46 %), the oracle on 0.3–1.5 % — consistent with the reported degradation of calibration under shift (Ovadia et al., AB).
* **H2 supported in the negative form, with a mechanism caveat.** M5 abstains on seen sources (claim .01–.05) and on **eqD** (.01–.04) but **not on eqC (.90–.94)**. eqA, eqB and eqD all yield *pure-noise observations* (x≡0); only eqC has a genuinely different observable signature (non-null response, lag τ<dt ≡ delay 0). The apparent "transfer" to eqD is therefore most plausibly the probe learning "no response ⇒ ambiguous" (shared signature), not a transferable notion of non-identifiability; with one non-null held-out source this is a single contrast, not a general law. M5 also pays on identifiable worlds: idnoisy claim rate falls but its wrong claims remain 0.38–0.43.
* **H3 not supported.** The intervention-aware learned-simulator agent (M7) does not recognise persistent ambiguity: its claim rate on eqC/eqD is .68–.95 (vs probes .80–.96), adaptivity reduces eqC claims only modestly (.89–.93 → .68–.87) and increases others; its *persistence* statistic (d_max over candidate interventions between the two leading class simulators) is **inverted**: AUROC for "unseen-equivalent abstentions have smaller d_max than identifiable abstentions" = 0.10–0.18 (n=56–165 vs 314–354), whereas the same statistic computed with exact simulators (oracle) gives 0.90–0.96. The learned simulators extrapolate outside their training mechanisms (b=0, τ<dt, delay 0) with arbitrary responses, so their predicted divergences are large exactly where the truth has none. The persistence flag also fires on 69–90 % of identifiable (`id`) abstentions. Hence imagination over interventions is only as reliable as the simulators' extrapolation.
* **H4 partially supported, conditional on the hypothesis class.** The structured oracle with a hypothesis class that contains the degenerate members abstains perfectly (claim ≤ .02 on eqC/eqD) and is far better calibrated under noise shift. But the same oracle with a grid **lacking** (delay 0, lag τ=0.05) claims on **67 %** of eqC worlds (median max-posterior 0.96): structure helps only if the class is right. On `approx` pairs the oracle's accuracy among claims is 0.61–0.75 although it claims 76–80 % (discrete grid vs continuous truth; state D at the grid level), and on out-of-class mechanisms it is confidently wrong (claim .90–.95 on ooc_osc) unless a goodness-of-fit flag is used (flags 100 % of ooc_osc in sweep, 68 % adaptive; ooc_sat 2–11 %, ooc_qd 1–29 %). The learned GOF flag (z² statistic of the learned simulators, 99th percentile of identifiable calibration) fires on 50–99 % of ooc_osc, 3–19 % of ooc_sat and 2–8 % of ooc_qd, and on 21–56 % of eqB/eqD worlds because the simulators fit "no response" poorly — abstention through *misfit*, not through recognising equivalence.
* **H5 (impossibility) – theory plus oracle illustration, not a new result.** `11` E2/E3 argue that finite-budget data cannot certify exact equivalence or class membership outside a declared family. Oracle budget curves (150 instances/category, adaptive EIG, 6/12/24 blocks): identifiable `id` claim rate .83/.97/.99, **budget-limited `idnoisy` .27/.59/.77 with accuracy .68/.81/.87**, exact `eqA/eqC/eqD` **0.00 at every budget** with posterior mass frozen (median max-post .57/.59/.33 at all budgets). Posterior stagnation under a doubled budget separates "needs more budget" from "no experiment in this interface helps" within a declared model class — it does not certify the latter, and the one-block d_max < 4 persistence statistic conflates them (the oracle flags 84–88 % of budget-limited abstentions as persistent).

## 4. Controls and audit
* Leakage audit (`results/epi_final/leak_audit.json`): gradient boosting on passive-block nuisance statistics (noise level, mean, variance, range) predicts equivalent-vs-identifiable at AUROC 0.52 (family A) and 0.48 (family B) within family. Family composition differs between categories by construction (eqA only family A; eqB, eqC only B) — recorded; probes see only latent features of (a,y), and eqD/eqC/id share the family mix with training. Episode length (48), action range and noise-stream construction are identical across categories (tests). Actions-only classifier earlier at chance for fixed policies.
* Benchmark neither trivial (probes lose on eqC) nor impossible (oracle claims 70–73 % of identifiable instances at 91–95 % accuracy).
* Conditional-simulator training: 3 seeds, 3000 steps (≈5 min each, CPU, 3 concurrent); final NLL ≈ −0.05…−0.10 (training mixture).

## 5. Failed / invalid / revised
* First guessed arXiv ids (3) pointed at unrelated papers; discarded. Smoke run (ns 11000, n≈40/category) was used only to debug and to inspect for code errors; it previewed the same qualitative picture, and the persistence-AUROC metric (threshold-free) was added after seeing it, before the final run (declared in `11`'s rule list implicitly through "reasons reported separately"; flagged here as a post-smoke addition).
* One-block persistence statistic (δ=4) is too weak: confuses budget-limited with equivalent even for the oracle; kept as pre-stated, reported as failed.
* Family VII (nested worlds) not implemented; Family II implemented only as protocol (passive start). Only **one** truly non-null held-out equivalence source (eqC); conclusions on "unseen structure" rest on it.

## 6. What is and is not established
Established (toy benchmark, development level): (i) standard calibration, conformal sets and ensembles over a prediction-trained latent do not detect exact non-identifiability from an unseen source; (ii) ambiguity-exposure helps only for sources with the same observable signature; (iii) learned class-conditional simulators used for intervention-aware persistence tests are unreliable out of class — their persistence statistic is inverted; (iv) exact-structure reasoning abstains correctly but only inside a hypothesis class that contains the degenerate members, and is overconfident on out-of-class and near-equivalent worlds without goodness-of-fit; (v) posterior stagnation under budget doubling separates budget-limited from exactly equivalent worlds for the oracle.
Not established: any positive transferable learned abstention; any result for latent causal variables, nested worlds, Dreamer/TD-MPC-scale models; novelty of any statement (E1–E4 are known flavours; literature on active system identification, Blackwell comparison and partial identification not read).
