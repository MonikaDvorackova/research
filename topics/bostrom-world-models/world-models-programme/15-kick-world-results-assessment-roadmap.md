# 15 — Can a learned world model discover that its environment's randomness is deterministic? Results, adversarial controls, Bostrom reading, internal review, roadmap

Scope: development experiments only; plan in `13`/`14` written before any kick-world run. Code: `src/eid/kick/`, `scripts/kick_*`. Raw: `results/kick_dev/`, `results/kick_long/`, `results/gwb_dev/rssm_diag/`. Instances (distinct generators) are the unit of replication: 3 per LFSR size, 2 per NLFSR size, 3 true-random controls; one training seed per instance. **No confidence intervals beyond per-instance values/ranges are claimed.** Exposed namespaces: instance generators 14000+, training streams 15000+, evaluation streams 16000+/17000+/18000+ (development; not confirmatory).

## 1. The prototype
**kick-world**: a partially observed nonlinear oscillator (hidden p, v, w; cubic restoring force; observations (p,v) with σ=0.02), driven by a hidden shock bit stream that is also observed as a binary *click*; the dynamics respond to the click. The bit stream comes from (i) true randomness (R), (ii) an n-bit **maximal LFSR** (random primitive taps, verified period 2^n−1), (iii) an n-bit **degree-2 NLFSR** (period ≥ 2^n/4). Actions are a smooth random control. Generators are never exposed. *Redesign recorded:* my first version inferred the bit from a mixture-density head and failed (inferred-bit accuracy 0.47 = chance, because mixture components collapse once the shock is predicted); replaced by the explicit click channel with an autoregressive GRU model p(click_t|past)·p(o_t|past,click_t). The first version's code path was removed; the smoke result (frac 0.51/z 305 at n=4) is preserved only in this note.
**Learned world model:** GRU latent (H=128; H=32 ablation), click BCE + Gaussian NLL on (p,v), Adam 2e-3, batch 64×96 steps. **Detection test (agent-computable, null-valid, P6):** frozen model, 512 fresh independent streams, per-stream mean click cross-entropy over t ≥ 16, one-sided z against ln 2; *frac* = (ln 2 − CE)/ln 2 = fraction of the shock-bit entropy the model removes. **Validity check:** the same model evaluated on fresh true-random shocks ("cross-R z") must not be positive. **Explanation:** a GF(2) recurrence (linear L=32, or degree-≤2 over the last min(n+2,14) bits) fitted to the *model's own click predictions*, then verified on fresh environment clicks (exact match on 40,960 bits ⇒ under H_rand probability 2^{−40960}, P7). **Baselines:** exact Berlekamp–Massey on the click stream (2n bits), degree-2 GF(2) polynomial fit, replay test (identical hidden seed and actions), finite-context MLP predictors (P5), true-random controls.

Validity: maximum cross-environment z over all 29 models = −0.36 (all ≤ 0): no model ever scored positive on true-random shocks. Capacity (H=32 vs 128, 4000 steps, instance 0): n=8: 0.106 vs 0.481; n=12: 0.001 vs 0.002 — smaller capacity slows learning.
**Optimisation vs capacity (5× longer training, 20,000 steps, instance 0 per size, H=128):** n=8 → 0.999 (z 1.1·10⁴), **exact verified explanation (linear and degree-2)**; n=10 → 0.978 (z 840), still rising, explanation not exact at 97.8 %; n=12 → 0.164 (z 28), still rising. Steps to reach 50 % of the entropy: ≈500 (n=4), ≈1,500 (n=6), ≈4,100 (n=8), ≈10,500 (n=10): a factor ≈ 2.6–3 per two extra bits (≈ 1.6–1.7× per bit); n=12 not reached by 20,000 steps. **So the n ≥ 10 failures at 4000 steps are optimisation/data limits, not capacity limits of H=128; the cost grows roughly exponentially with n.** Extrapolating this 3-point trend to n=16 (≈10⁵–10⁶ steps) is speculation, not a result.
**Exact structured comparison:** Berlekamp–Massey identifies every LFSR from 2n bits (8–32 bits for n=4…16) with certainty, i.e. about six orders of magnitude fewer observations than the learned model needs even at n=8 (4·10³ steps × 64 × 96 ≈ 2.5·10⁷ observation-steps for 50 %). For the NLFSR, BM needs 160–404 bits (linear complexity ≫ n) while a degree-2 GF(2) fit succeeds with O(L²) equations when the class is declared — the same data are easy or hard depending on the hypothesis language (P4).

## 3. The interface asymmetry (P2) measured
Replay test (identical hidden seed and actions, 256 episodes × 48 clicks): click agreement = **1.000 for every deterministic generator** (LFSR n=4…16, NLFSR n=6…10, both H) and 0.497–0.509 for true randomness, i.e. exact detection with two episodes, for all n, including n=16 where the learned model has z ≈ 1. This is a consequence of the construction (the generator restarts from the recorded seed) and is exactly the reset intervention that embedded observers inside our world do not have. It is the *only* generator-independent detector here; it requires no learning.

## 4. Provable memory threshold tested (P5) — `results/kick_dev/window_control.json`
MLP predictors with context k clicks. Exact Bayes-optimal CE over a full period (window property): k<n → ln 2 − ε with ε = 0.011 (n=6,k=5), 0.0027 (n=8,k=7), 0.0006 (n=10,k=9), ≈ 10⁻⁴ (n=12,k=11); learned MLPs match these values (e.g. n=8,k=7: 0.6905 vs exact 0.6904). For k ≥ n, CE = 0.000 exactly (learned, 3000 steps, **including n=12**). Hence (i) context < n yields an exponentially small advantage no matter the optimiser; (ii) a supervised MLP given context ≥ n solves n=12 in ≈3000 steps (≈10⁷ click-steps), whereas the GRU world model has not by 20,000 steps — **a task-specific predictor with the right context beats the generic world model**; the world model's difficulty is *finding* which part of its history matters, not computing XOR.

## 5. RSSM-vs-GRU diagnostic (Section 9)
Controlled variants (gwb benchmark, seed 200, 2000 steps, identical data; excess prior-predictive NLL on uniform actions | 90 % coverage): current RSSM 0.265 | 0.77; **decode from h only 0.129 | 0.87**; Dreamer-style free-bits 1.0 + balance 0.5/0.1 **3.355 | 0.48**; no prior-predictive loss 250.7 | 0.09; β=5 0.237 | 0.79; H=256 0.243 | 0.78; **decode-from-h + Dreamer-style free bits 0.114 | 0.89**; deterministic GRU 0.112 | 0.91. Raw posterior KL: 1.3 (current), 0.54 (h-only), 5.9 (Dreamer-style free bits). **Diagnosis:** the gap is an objective/latent-bottleneck effect — the decoder reads the observation noise through the posterior sample (a noise-autoencoding shortcut); with independent observation noise a stochastic latent carries no structure and only hurts prediction. Capacity (H=256) and KL weight (β=5) do not close it; removing the shortcut does. Not an implementation bug beyond the (previously fixed) missing prior-predictive loss. Deviations from DreamerV3 (TH): Gaussian vs categorical latents, free bits 0.1 vs 1 nat, balance 0.8/0.2 vs β_dyn=1/β_rep=0.1, no symlog/unimix. TD-MPC2 (TH) is decoder-free (joint-embedding) and would not exhibit this specific shortcut; I did not run it.

## 6. Adversarial controls — what survives
* *Simple supervised baseline solves it?* **Yes**, given the right context (MLP with k ≥ n, §4) and exact algebra solves it with 2n bits. The world model is neither necessary nor efficient for this question.
* *Structured Bayesian/algebraic baseline?* BM (declared linear class) and the degree-2 fit dominate in sample cost by orders of magnitude; both fail outside their class (BM on NLFSR).
* *Fixed intervention policy matches learned planner?* Not tested here (actions are random; the click process is not action-dependent). **This is a design limitation: the "active discovery" component of the brief is absent from the selected experiment.**
* *Result disappears on unseen mechanisms?* Only two generator types (LFSR, NLFSR) and one world; no out-of-family generator (e.g. LCG, xorshift with multiplication, cryptographic PRG) was tested.
* *Matched predictive accuracy / compute?* Compute matched across instances (4000 steps); not matched against alternative architectures (no transformer, no RSSM on this task).
* *Artifacts?* The click is an explicit observed channel, so the "world" adds nothing to the detection problem beyond a distractor and a (p,v) NLL term; the experiment is essentially bit-stream prediction with a GRU. Period memorisation is learning for n ≤ 12 (period ≤ 4095) and cannot be separated from rule learning here.
* *Non-computational explanation?* Any short deterministic recurrence reproducing the clicks is observationally equivalent to a chaotic-but-finite physical process: the verified explanation is level 3–4 (mechanism sufficient for the events), not "computer".
* *Privileged information?* Explanations use observed clicks only; the evaluator's only privileged inputs are labels of generator type used for reporting and the minimal BM bit counts. Hypothesis generation is **limited to fitting expressions in a declared GF(2) language** — not open-ended discovery.
* *Calibration/validity:* no false positive in 32 model×environment evaluations on true randomness (z ≤ 0.2); the explanation test is exact.

## 7. Bostrom
The experiment concerns one concrete class of "computational signature": determinism of the environment's own randomness. It shows (a) detectability is a *three-way* notion — information-theoretically trivial from ≈ n+log|𝒢| bits (P1), interface-dependent (a reset/replay intervention gives certainty from two episodes, P2), and computationally graded for a bounded learner (here a roughly exponential-in-n learning cost, with a hard memory threshold at k = n); (b) the only signature that scales to arbitrarily large n is the one requiring an intervention we cannot perform on our own universe; (c) under a cryptographic PRG assumption (P3, conditional, not tested) a bounded observer without replay has no access at all. Bostrom's argument is silent on detectability: a simulation with true randomness, or with a PRG too strong for the observers' resources, is observationally compatible with its negation. Nothing here bears on the probability that we are simulated, and the verified recurrence is *not* evidence of an implementation (chaos/finite-state physics is equivalent). Learned world models change the practical frontier (n ≈ 6–10 at this budget, larger with more data) without touching the fundamental limits.

## 8. Internal critical review (not external peer review)
* **Strongest rejection reason:** the measured phenomenon (neural nets learn small LFSRs; cost grows steeply; larger state fails; MLP with right context succeeds; exact algebra is vastly cheaper) is predicted by known theory/prior experiments (Tao et al. 2025 AB; Boancă SD; SQ/parity learning NA; Yao/Blum–Micali SD). The embedded-observer framing adds a wrapper and an explicit-click channel that makes the problem equivalent to sequence prediction.
* **Statistical weakness:** 2–3 instances per cell, one seed, per-run values only; the exponential-cost trend rests on four points (n=4,6,8,10) at one instance per long run.
* **Scope weakness:** no action-dependent generator, no hidden (unobserved) event channel, no transformer/RSSM/TD-MPC2 comparison, no out-of-family generator, no cryptographic-strength generator.
* **What is solid:** null-validity of the detector (proved P6 + 0 false positives), exact verification of explanations (P7), the replay asymmetry as measured, the P5 threshold values (exact), the RSSM diagnosis (controlled, single seed).

## 9. Decision and originality
**REVISE.** No genuinely original scientific contribution has been identified. The defensible new-to-this-programme content is the *integrated, quantified separation* (information-theoretic / interface / bounded-learner) with null-valid agent-computable detection and exact verification; its novelty relative to the cryptography/learning-theory literature is unverified (that literature was not read) and, by the threat matrix in `13`, probably small. A GO would require a result not predicted by P1–P7 and prior art.

## 10. Roadmap (multi-month, evidence-gated)
* **M1 (≤1 month, no compute):** read full texts: Tao et al. 2025, the PCG paper, Boancă, Gohr 2019; Yao/Blum–Micali/Goldwasser–Micali; SQ lower bounds for parity; Le Cam and Blackwell; Chalmers. Output: revised threat matrix; **gate: stop if the interface-asymmetry and bounded-learner frontier are already stated in the literature.**
* **M2 (1–2 months):** make the click *unobserved* (only its effect on the dynamics is visible), add an action-dependent generator (actions gate state advancement) so interventions matter for detection; compare RSSM/TD-MPC2-style/transformer with the GRU and with the window-MLP baseline at matched compute. Question to answer: does an active planner that selects *which actions expose the generator* beat random actions? (the part of the brief not yet tested).
* **M3 (2–3 months):** scaling law for detection cost vs n with ≥5 instances per size and several seeds; n up to 16–20 needs ≈ 10⁵–10⁶ steps (4000 steps ≈ 4 min locally ⇒ roughly 2–70 h per run; the largest sizes may justify a GPU — **no cloud resources are used or requested now**).
* **M4:** out-of-family generators (LCG, xorshift*, PCG, a toy cryptographic PRG) and an observer-class sweep (finite context, capacity, data); test P3 empirically by checking that a cryptographic-style PRG remains undetected at budgets where LFSR(n) is learned.
* **M5:** philosophical paper section on the evidential limits: levels 1–6 and the replay-intervention asymmetry; no empirical claim about our universe.
* **Preregistered confirmation only after M1–M2 and only if a surprising effect exists** (reserved seed ranges: 600–629, 700–719, 800–829, plus new 19000+).
