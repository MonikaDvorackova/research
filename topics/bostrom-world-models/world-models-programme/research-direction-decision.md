# Research direction decision (synthesis phase)

Inputs: `research-synthesis-and-evidence-audit.md`, `identifiability-and-embedded-observers.md`, `literature-and-novelty-map.md`. No experiment was run. Internal assessment; not peer review.

## 1. Candidate questions
**Q1 — Interface-relative identifiability hierarchy for embedded observers.** For classes of controlled hidden-state mechanisms and interfaces I = (admissible interventions, reset structure, reach restriction), which properties of a mechanism are identifiable in the limit, and what strict hierarchy do the interfaces *passive single trajectory ⊂ interventional single trajectory ⊂ episodic with reset ⊂ counterfactual replay* induce? Which structural premises (recurrence/ergodicity, analyticity, finite memory) close the gaps between reachable-region data and global behaviour? Computational refinement by a second axis (bounded distinguishers).
**Q2 — Resource-matched computational indistinguishability of implementation features.** Which implementation features of a host (randomness source, shortcuts, finite precision) are visible to an observer whose resources are bounded by those of the host, and what must an argument that proposes to detect a simulation assume about the simulator?
**Q3 — Representation sufficiency under restricted policy support.** When does a latent state learned under a policy class Π₀ retain the information needed to answer interventional queries outside Π₀ (PSR minimality, causal abstraction, bisimulation), and what is the quantitative price when it does not?

## 3. Selected primary direction: Q1 — with an explicit literature gate
**Precise question.** For the class 𝓜_fin of finite-state controlled hidden-Markov mechanisms (finite S, A, O; computable kernels) and interface levels L0 passive single trajectory, L1 interventional single trajectory, L2 interventional with episodic reset, L3 counterfactual replay: for each level, (a) what is the interventional-equivalence quotient actually identifiable in the limit (reliable identification à la Gold/Kelly), (b) which structural premises make reachable-region behaviour determine global behaviour, and (c) how does (a) change when the observer is restricted to polynomially bounded distinguishers (class-2 phenomena)?
**Distinction from known results.** Known per component: behavioural equivalence/realization (Singh et al.); interventions refine equivalence (Hauser–Bühlmann; CHT); identification in the limit and its topology (Gold; Kelly, via SEP); computational barriers (Aaronson §7.1; Berthet–Rigollet); embedded-inference impossibilities (Wolpert). Not found in what I accessed: a single hierarchy of *embedded interface levels* for *hidden-state controlled systems* with the reset/reach restrictions treated as the independent variable. **This distinction is unverified**; the literature gate must test it.
**Why it matters.** It converts the vague "can an embedded observer identify the rules of its world?" into a table of provable statements — which questions have answers at all, for whom, with which premises — and it would supply the missing mathematical content of philosophical claims about empirical equivalence (underdetermination, simulation detection). The programme's toy results cannot supply this; theory can.
**Proposed contribution.** A theorem table with proofs/counterexamples for 𝓜_fin, classifying each (property, interface, resource) cell into the five classes of the framework, with explicit assumptions. If one separation is not a corollary of cited work (for example a strictness result between L1 and L2 for hidden-state systems with reach restriction, or a premise that exactly closes the gap), it is the contribution; otherwise the output is a short expository clarification (framework + classification + worked counterexamples) or nothing.
**Explicit assumptions.** Finite-state mechanisms; known A, O; policies may be randomised and adaptive; success = identification in the limit (primary) and finite-sample/minimax (secondary); resources as polynomial-time distinguishers (secondary).
**Success criterion.** (i) Every cell of the table has a statement with a source or a proof; (ii) at least one proposition is **not derivable** from the cited results, certified by (a) a documented failure to find it in the sources of the literature gate and (b) an independent mathematical check (a human specialist or a second proof strategy); (iii) the proposition is non-trivial (not a restatement of P2/P3).
**Failure criterion.** After the literature gate, every statement is a corollary of known results → stop the line; at most write a 3–5 page expository note, and only if it adds clarity not already in Aaronson/Kelly/Singh et al.
**Realistic next milestone (literature gate L3, ≈ 6–8 weeks of reading, no code).** Read in full, and record accessed/not accessed: Gold 1967; Angluin (queries); Kelly 1996 ch. 3 and Kelly–Schulte; Jaeger 2000; Hsu–Kakade–Zhang 2012; Jin et al. 2020; Mossel–Roch 2005; Kearns–Valiant 1994; Manchak 2009, Malament 1977, Glymour 1977, Earman; Shpitser–Pearl 2006 and the CHT proof; Ryabko on ergodic testing; Bickel–Ritov–Rydén 1998. Output: theorem-by-theorem map for 𝓜_fin and a go/stop decision on the "unverified" distinction. Obtain at least one external reader (a specialist in learning theory or philosophy of science).

## 4. Is another experiment justified? No.
The next contribution should be a **mathematical/conceptual** one (theorem table, possibly an impossibility or separation result), grounded in existing theory. No experiment is warranted now: every available experiment either demonstrates a known fact (coverage amplification, predictive ≠ structural, equation-error bottleneck, PRF hardness) or has an outcome fixed by its construction (a designed vocabulary or generator). Rejected designs, with reasons: (1) any further agent/benchmark/neural/symbolic-regression pipeline — outcome determined by closure alignment and by baseline configuration; (2) replications of coverage amplification — known; (3) "does a learned world model discover X" — architecture-specific, no bearing on identifiability; (4) a confirmatory study of any phase-22 hypothesis — the hypotheses were refuted or are tooling-dependent.
**Conditional design (only if the theory yields a quantitative, non-obvious prediction).** *Hypothesis*: for a random ensemble of finite-state controlled mechanisms, the number of ≈_I-classes consistent with a reachable-region sample, and the minimal information-theoretic experiment length, differ across interface levels L0–L3 by the amounts the theorem table predicts. *Method*: exact enumeration of equivalence classes by minimisation of the system-dynamics matrix (no learning, no neural network, no fitted parameters). *Independent variable*: interface level and reach restriction; *outcome*: class counts and exact separating-experiment length; *baselines*: the closed-form predictions from the theorem table and from known bounds (Le Cam two-point bound; coverage lower bound T4); *external benchmark*: random Mealy/HMM ensembles from the minimal-realization and automata-learning literature, not generated by the author's agent code. *Falsifier*: observed counts or lengths contradict a proved prediction (indicating a bug or a false theorem) or a conjectured separation fails on an enumerated counterexample. *Why either result matters*: a counterexample prunes a conjecture; agreement is only a sanity check and should not be written up as a finding. This is a theorem-testing tool, not an experiment that can establish anything on its own.

## 5. Directions discontinued (with reasons)
1. **Autonomous symbolic-regression / structure-discovery agents** (docs 18–25): demonstrated selection from supplied vocabularies; always-on search ≥ adaptive loop; equation-error pipelines limited by state reconstruction; none addresses identifiability of embedded observers.
2. **Learned intervention planners and meta-trained world models as discovery engines** (docs 16–17): no advantage over fixed designs; negative for GRU/RSSM at this scale; theory predicts none outside misspecification pathologies.
3. **Learned latent representations as a route to mechanism identification** (docs 07–12, 20, 24): coverage/architecture-limited; not essential to the strongest question (see framework §8).
4. **Certified-HMM-likelihood tooling** (phase 11): closed; published methods exist; off the central question.
5. **Further "price of validity" empirical work** (E6′): theory reduces to known bounds; confirmatory run not authorised and not warranted.
6. **Simulation-detection experiments with toy simulators**: results depend on arbitrary assumptions about the simulator (Beane et al. state theirs); toy outcomes carry no information about our universe.

## 6. Unresolved uncertainties
1. Whether Q1's hierarchy over embedded interface levels is already contained in formal learning theory (Gold, Angluin, Kelly), in the causal-discovery-with-interventions literature, or in the philosophy of cosmology (Manchak/Malament) — not read.
2. Whether hidden-state, reach-restricted controlled systems admit an interesting separation between single-trajectory interventional and episodic access beyond ergodicity/recurrence premises.
3. Whether the "identifiable but intractable" class has sharper, known results for controlled HMM/POMDP learning (Mossel–Roch; Jin et al.) than the PRF archetype used here.
4. Correct treatment of coarse-graining (effective laws at finite resolution) inside the equivalence framework.
5. The accessibility of the outer-interface assumption: any simulator-independent test of simulation is, by P2/P7, unlikely; this is argued, not proved, and depends on what counts as the interface.
6. Several earlier novelty statements rested on abstract-level reading; they are not reversed here but should not be relied on.
7. All earlier "independent reviews" were by the same agent; no external review of any result exists.
8. Seed-range accounting: 600–629 were consumed by X1; later statements to the contrary are corrected in the audit.
9. This synthesis read targeted passages of the primary sources through text extraction; proofs were not independently re-derived.

## 7. Verdict
**REFRAME.** The original motivation — what an observer embedded in a generative system can know about it — remains valuable and is mathematically tractable once reframed as interface- and resource-relative identifiability of the effective-law class; the implementation line that dominated phases 13–22 neither answered it nor can, and should stop. The reframed question may turn out to be already answered by formal learning theory and cryptographic learning limits; the literature gate decides, and **STOP** is the correct outcome if every statement there is a corollary of known results.
