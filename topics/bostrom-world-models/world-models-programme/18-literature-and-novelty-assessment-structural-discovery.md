## 2. What is already established (so it will not be claimed as new)
* Equation discovery by sparse regression or evolutionary search over a library/grammar, with controls and noise (1–8).
* Ensemble-variance active data collection for SINDy inside a declared library, state measured (4).
* A learned latent coordinate system with sparse symbolic dynamics for passive data (6).
* EIG/discrimination experiment design for declared models, and its documented fragility under misspecification (10, 11, 12, SD).
* Interventions required to identify nonlinear structure; input-design for system identification (SINDYc future-work remark; Wagenmaker–Jamieson, FT in `16`).

## 3. What the closest work does **not** (in the passages read) provide
No source read combines, in one loop, (a) **misfit-based rejection of the current structural class** with a calibrated test, (b) **expansion of the hypothesis language** to new structure types (delay, thresholds, memory, hidden-state feedback) rather than new coefficients, (c) **experiment choice aimed at falsifying the leading structural hypothesis** (disagreement among *structurally different* executable hypotheses, not ensemble variance of one model), and (d) **explicit abstention** in two distinct modes (experimentally equivalent hypotheses; out-of-language mechanism) — evaluated on instances from structural families the learner was not given. Each ingredient exists; the *composition and its evaluation under interventions with held-out structure* is the unread-literature gap. This is a **gap in the sources I read, not a proof of novelty**: items in row 14 and the broader symbolic-discovery, robot-scientist and model-discrimination literatures are unread and could contain it.

## 4. Threat ranking
1. **Model discrimination / T-optimal design (SD, row 12) and active E-SINDy (FT, row 4):** both choose experiments by disagreement among rival models; my "falsification-driven selection" may simply re-implement classical discrimination design with a richer hypothesis set. *Mitigation:* compare against EIG/BED and ensemble-variance baselines and report honestly; claim only the evaluated composition.
2. **SINDy-autoencoders (FT, row 6) for the representation role:** a latent + symbolic method exists; my experiment tests only whether a *learned state estimator* helps the *proposal* step relative to analytic reconstruction, a much narrower question.
3. **PySR (software not run) and AI Feynman:** my grammar search is a restricted enumerative stand-in, **not** a PySR replacement; a PySR baseline is a declared gap.
4. **BED misspecification pathologies (FT, row 10):** these *motivate* criticism-before-design; they also predict that the declared-class BED baseline will fail out of class (already observed in `17`).

## 5. Consequence for the research question
The open mission question is narrowed to: *given an interface of admissible interventions and a hypothesis language of executable structural primitives, does a loop of misfit criticism → structure expansion → hypothesis-discriminating intervention recover, or correctly refuse to recover, mechanisms from structurally unfamiliar families more efficiently and more reliably than (i) SINDYc, (ii) generic NARX identification, (iii) a nonparametric static-map baseline, (iv) declared-class BED, and (v) its own ablations — and does a learned latent state estimator improve the proposal step?* Hypothesis, protocol and decision rules: `19`. Prior expectation (from rows 4, 6, 10 and `17`): in-language gains from structure search over SINDYc/NARX are likely; gains of design over a good fixed sweep are small; a learned estimator is unlikely to beat an analytic one because state reconstruction here is mechanism-independent.
