# 25 — Scientific assessment and next decision (Phase 22)

Evidence: docs 22 (literature/baselines), 23 (pre-registered protocol), 24 (experiments). Internal analysis; development-stage; no confirmatory run was made or is authorised.

## 1. Answer to the research question
*Can an agent recognise that its hypothesis language is insufficient, construct a new executable structural hypothesis, and validate it on a previously unseen mechanism?* **Partly, and not by the route proposed.**
* **Recognise insufficiency: yes where misfit is visible.** Calibrated criticism (false rejection 0.00 on the dev control, ≤ 0.17 on correct calibration families) rejected L0 on every mechanism whose L0 approximation fails (`rectsq`, `leak`, `bilin`, `quadlag`, `rate`, `gdelay`, and `saw`). **No** test of this kind can detect inadequacy when a wrong-structured L0 hypothesis already predicts within noise (`sin`, `bump`, `sqrtabs`: rejection 0.0–0.1): that is predictive near-equivalence (P2), and it caps the power of any criticism-triggered scheme (measured power 0.42–0.78 < the 0.8 target).
* **Construct a new executable hypothesis: yes, inside a designer-specified closure.** Predictive recovery 0.94–1.00 at B ≥ 8 versus 0.57–0.90 for fixed-language search and ≤ 0.40 for every equation-discovery or system-identification baseline in realistic configurations (SINDYc, weak/implicit SINDy, NARX, Hammerstein, PySR fixed/expanded on a reconstructed state, PySR on raw observations). **But the advantage is not the adaptive expansion**: an always-on search of the same closure (`static_all`) is as good on prediction and *better* on structure (L2 0.70–0.78 vs 0.38–0.44 on the holdout; paired −0.32 to −0.34 [−0.40, −0.24]) at lower wall-clock. Structure is recovered in only 0.38–0.55 of identifiable cases by the full loop; 0.42–0.62 of its successes are predictive without being structural; about 40 % of its claims have the wrong structure.
* **The stage vocabularies are not discovery.** `sin`, `bump`, `sqrtabs`, `relu∘sq`, `lp`, `rate`, `gate∘delay`, `sq∘lp` and `x₁x₂` were supplied by the designer; the agent selects among them. This is a larger fixed grammar searched in stages, not the introduction of previously unavailable operators, and the loop's outcome on `saw` (a sawtooth no vocabulary contains) shows what it does there: it abstains (10/10), with a predictive surrogate of RMSE 0.27 and no claim.
* **Validate by discriminating experiments: no demonstrated benefit.** The falsify design is not better than a fixed sweep or random programmes (the sign flips between splits) and costs 4–5× more compute; ensemble-variance design (neural intervention prediction) is worst.

## 2. Hypotheses (doc 23 rules)
* **H1** (primary): *supported only in a weak form.* L1 and L2 advantages over the realistic baselines hold with CIs excluding 0; the pre-registered downgrade clause fires because the static broad grammar matches/beats the loop. Moreover the baselines' weakness is largely an interface artefact: PySR with the privileged hidden state reaches L1 0.65 (dev) / 0.80 (holdout) at B=12, so the agent's lead over equation-error methods mostly reflects *simulation-based (output-error) fitting without state reconstruction*. **Claim "adaptive expansion beats fixed-language symbolic regression and system identification": not supported.**
* **H2** (learned latents): not supported; removed from the recommended architecture (improvement over a tuned Kalman front end is split-dependent: +0.17 [0.05,0.30] dev, +0.04 [−0.10,0.16] holdout for PySR; negative for SINDYc on dev; stage-ordering effects nil).
* **H3** (falsification efficiency): not supported.
* **H4** (criticism): false-rejection criterion met; power criterion failed; AUROC 0.90–0.94 on dev was never a sufficient indicator.
* **Non-identifiable and beyond-closure behaviour** is the one clean positive: null-equivalent explanations claimed or abstained in 0.8–1.0 of non-identifiable instances; `saw` abstained 10/10.

## 3. Mathematical status
P1 (power depends on whether the collected programmes excite the missing structure), P2 (predictive equivalence on collected data does not imply structural equivalence; expansion does not guarantee discovery) and P3 (Le Cam/disagreement design score, misspecification limits) are elementary and either known or immediate; no theorem is claimed. P2 acquired an empirical instantiation: `abs+sq` vs `relu(x²−θ)`, `dz+tanh` vs `sin`/`bump`/`sqrt` on a 0.9 m actuator range, with RMSE ≤ 0.05 and wrong structure in 0.4–0.6 of runs. A lower bound separating criticism-triggered from always-on model comparison was **not** derived: the empirical result (blind spot of size = predictively imitable mechanisms) suggests the formal statement "criticism power ≤ 1 − (mass of mechanisms with ε < noise-detectable gap)", which I have not proven and which is close to the definition of non-detectability; no originality is claimed. Literature for hypothesis-class expansion with model selection (BIC/MDL across languages — the Bayesian machine scientist line, Guimerà et al.; not accessed) very likely contains the always-on result.

## 4. Novelty assessment
No component is new (doc 22). The composition is not supported as an advantage by the evidence: the strongest simple alternative (broad grammar + BIC with a vocabulary penalty, or PySR with a good state) matches it. The surviving, modest contributions are measurements: (i) a frozen, hash-pinned benchmark with L1/L2/L3 success levels and a beyond-closure and two non-identifiable controls; (ii) the quantified blind spot of calibrated misfit tests; (iii) the diagnosis that equation-error pipelines on a reconstructed state are limited by state error rather than operator libraries (oracle-state PySR 0.65–0.80 vs 0.03–0.28); (iv) the diagnosis of earlier SINDYc divergence as library collinearity plus derivative noise; (v) a documented misspecification pathology of joint maximum-likelihood Kalman tuning.

## 5. Relation to Bostrom and embedded observers (kept separate)
Whether an observer can find a mechanism absent from its vocabulary: here, only if a designer put it in a larger closure; the sole case outside any closure produced a calibrated "my language fails" without a replacement. Whether interventions reveal hidden structure: yes — the u2 channel carried the skeleton, large-u1 programmes the coupling; removing u2 from the pool cost structure (0.54 → 0.38), not prediction. Observational equivalence: two kinds were measured — exact (`thresh`, `bumpfar`, claimed null) and soft (`sin` vs `dz+tanh`, 0.4–0.6 of runs predictively adequate but structurally wrong); soft equivalence is the practical limit, and it is a limit of the *interface and noise level*, not of the model class. Computational cost: the broader the closure, the more candidates (540 → 3 500), but not decisively more wall-clock; the toy observer can reset and replay worlds, which an embedded observer cannot. Distinguishing "my theory is wrong" from "the mechanism is inaccessible": calibrated misfit can flag the former (saw) but cannot separate it from the latter when a predictive near-equivalent exists. None of this bears on whether our universe is simulated; successful equation discovery in a simulator the author wrote says nothing about simulators, and a model of reality is not its implementation.

## 6. Limitations (summary)
Benchmark and agent share an author and closure; PySR/SINDy are my equation-error pipelines (tuned on a separate namespace), a simulation-based PySR is missing and would be the decisive baseline; neither Julia-based AI-Descartes, ProGED, DSR nor transformer SR were run; 10 instances per family; GRU seeds not in the CIs; holdout run once but not independent; all namespaces used are consumed; structural signature for PySR is a token heuristic; noise/stress tests have 24 instances per cell without CIs; stiff and stacked mechanisms untested; the 'wrong-structure' criterion is strict (lin L2 0.5–0.7).

## 7. Decision
**NO-GO** for the proposed architecture — criticism-triggered adaptive language expansion, falsification-driven design and learned latent representations as a distinct route to out-of-language discovery: it is not better than its simpler static-grammar ablation on structure, not better on prediction, and the learned-latent and design components add nothing that survives replication across splits. This is not a refutation of the broader problem: the benchmark, the blind-spot result and the state-error diagnosis are reusable. No confirmatory study is justified.
**If the problem is continued** (evidence-gated, development only): (1) build a simulation-based (output-error) symbolic-regression baseline and rerun the matched comparison — it is the one comparator that could overturn the "lead over PySR" reading; (2) replace designer vocabularies by an independent operator generator (random compositions of a calculus atom set, mechanisms drawn by a separate script not read by the agent) so that closure alignment can be measured as a variable rather than assumed; (3) test grammar *growth* rather than selection (operators synthesised from residual structure) against `saw`-like mechanisms; (4) stop work on learned latents and designed interventions unless a regime (partial observability that defeats the Kalman front end) is found where they win.
