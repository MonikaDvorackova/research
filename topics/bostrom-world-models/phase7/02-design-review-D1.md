# Design review D1

**Reviewer status.** This review was conducted by the same agent that wrote the proposal, adversarially, with exact calculations. It is **not independent**. A human or independent-agent review is still outstanding and is recorded as an open item; D1 below is "passed (self-review) after revision".

**Object reviewed.** `phase6/08-next-experiment-prereg.md` (proposal), then the revised design in `03-preregistration-X1.md`.

## 0. Summary of defects found in the Phase 6 proposal
| # | Defect | How found | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1.1 | The proposal's test used a **saturated order-2 alternative**. Its learning regret (parameters × ln t) exceeds the signal: exact KL of the truths to the nearest class member is 8×10⁻⁴ to 3×10⁻² nats/episode (`results/phase7/feasibility_seed500.txt`). | Exact enumeration | H3 (power ≥ 0.8 within 2000 episodes at ε = 0.5) was **infeasible for any test with that alternative** and probably for the oracle LR test at ε = 0.2 (N* ≈ 2900 episodes). |
| D1.2 | M contained t.02 (a transition-perturbed member) which absorbs the T_out family. | Exact KL table | Class not "clean"; removed. |
| D1.3 | The criticism design P4 would have had access to a family W that the within-class design P3 did not: **information confound** between "criticism target" and "access to alternatives". | Review item 8 | Added P5 (within-class EIG over M ∪ W), same information as P4. |
| D1.4 | Median episodes-to-reject under heavy censoring is unstable. | Dev data | Primary effect size changed to restricted mean time (RMST). |
| D1.5 | My prediction that unanticipated deviations are undetectable was **wrong** (dev: t10 is rejected 70% by random intervention). The exact reason: the power of the e-process is governed by the margin KL(truth‖M) − KL(truth‖M∪W), which is small but positive for t10 (3.33×10⁻² − 2.85×10⁻²) and negligible for t05 (8.61×10⁻³ − 8.17×10⁻³). | Dev data + exact table | H6 replaced by exact margin-based predictions; reported as exploratory characterisation. |

These changes were made after development runs on seeds 500–505 and **before** any confirmatory data existed.

## 1. Type-I error under optional stopping and adaptive collection (items 1, 4, 5)
**Claim (Proposition X1-V).** Let M be a finite set of controlled processes fixed before data, W any further finite set, K = |M ∪ W|, α ∈ (0, 1). A run produces a history h_t = (a₁, o₁, …, a_t, o_t) (all episodes concatenated, with a fixed episode structure). Let π be **any** action rule with a_t chosen from h_{t−1} and independent internal randomness (adaptive, randomised, depending on posteriors over M ∪ W). For a component k write p_k(o | h_{t−1}, a_t) for its one-step predictive conditional (computed by exact filtering inside the episode and a reset at episode starts) and p_k(D_t) = ∏_{i≤t} p_k(o_i | h_{i−1}, a_i). Define
 E_t = ( K⁻¹ Σ_k p_k(D_t) ) / max_{m∈M} p_m(D_t).
If the data are generated by some θ* ∈ M under π, then P_{θ*,π}(∃ t: E_t ≥ 1/α) ≤ α. In particular the rule "reject the first time E_t ≥ 1/α" has type-I error ≤ α for every stopping rule, including optional stopping and continuation.

**Proof.** The joint probability of the realised history under (θ*, π) factorises as ∏ π(a_i | h_{i−1}) · p_{θ*}(o_i | h_{i−1}, a_i). Let q(D_t) = K⁻¹ Σ_k p_k(D_t). Then q(D_t)/q(D_{t−1}) = Σ_k w_k(t−1) p_k(o_t | h_{t−1}, a_t) with w_k(t−1) ∝ p_k(D_{t−1}); i.e. q is a predictable conditional law (depends on h_{t−1}, a_t only). Define L_t = q(D_t) / p_{θ*}(D_t), L₀ = 1. Then
 E[L_t | h_{t−1}, a_t] = L_{t−1} Σ_{o: p_{θ*}(o|·)>0} p_{θ*}(o | ·) · q(o | ·)/p_{θ*}(o | ·) = L_{t−1} Σ_{o: p_{θ*}>0} q(o | ·) ≤ L_{t−1}.
The policy's factor π(a_t | h_{t−1}) does not enter the ratio, so L_t is a nonnegative supermartingale in the filtration generated by (h_t, internal randomisation). Because θ* ∈ M, max_{m∈M} p_m(D_t) ≥ p_{θ*}(D_t), hence E_t ≤ L_t pointwise. By Ville's inequality P(∃ t: L_t ≥ 1/α) ≤ α L₀ = α. ∎

**What this uses and what it does not.**
- It uses **the actual construction**, not the universal-inference theorem. The paper's Theorem 9 (read) is stated for i.i.d. data; the argument above is the same martingale argument but with conditional likelihoods, and I verified its ingredients rather than citing it.
- The **composite null** M is handled by the max in the denominator, not by calibration. No calibration data exist: M, W and the policy are fixed before any data; the alternative is a prequentially-updated mixture (predictable). There is no train/evaluation split because there is no fitted parameter.
- **The policy does not change the null bound**: it changes the null distribution of D_t but the bound holds uniformly over π. What the policy changes is the *power*.
- Requirements that could fail: (a) π must not depend on θ* or on hidden states — enforced by the Sampler interface and by a source-inspection test; (b) M must be fixed in advance; (c) the episode reset must be independent of the past (true in the benchmark); (d) the truth must be in M, with the same *interaction protocol* (actions, observation alphabet). A null truth with a different horizon or a non-stationary truth is outside the claim.

**Computational checks** (`tests/test_x1.py`): (i) every component's likelihood of an adaptively collected history sums to 1 over observation sequences, so q is a proper law and the ratio is a supermartingale (exact enumeration, T = 3, adaptive rule depending on past observations); (ii) split-state truth has KL = 0 against E0 (exact); (iii) a null smoke run never rejects; (iv) deterministic given streams; (v) the observer code never references the truth. **Empirical**: dev pooled null rejection 47/2880 = 0.016 (≤ 0.05), no NaN in 7200 runs; max log E over null runs 3.69 vs threshold ln 20 = 3.00 (rejections happen, as expected, but at ≤ 5%).

## 2. Validity of the statistic (item 2) and the null as composite (item 3)
Covered by §1. Note the test is valid for the null "the interaction kernel *on the actions taken* equals some member's". It is therefore valid but **not consistent against every alternative**: it has power only where the alternative mixture contains something closer to the truth than the class, up to regret (§3). A truth equal to a member's law on the probed class (e.g. the bisimilar split-state control, or t05/t10 under policies that never use action 2) is *in the null* for that class.

## 3. Power (item 6)
**Exact rate.** Under a stationary policy with per-episode KL rates d_M = min_{m∈M} KL(truth‖m) and d_A = min_{k∈M∪W} KL(truth‖k), E[log E_t] grows at the rate d_M − d_A per episode, minus an O(ln K) offset (the mixture weight 1/K). So detection by this test needs N·(d_M − d_A) ≳ ln K + ln(1/α) ≈ 6.8 nats for K = 46 and α = 0.05 (an approximation; the real crossing is stochastic). Table (seed 500, wide policy q = 1/3; `results/phase7/feasibility_seed500.txt`):

| truth | d_M | d_A | margin | episodes ≈ 6.8/margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pc0 / pc1 / pc2 (anticipated) | 1.7–2.4 ×10⁻² | 0 | = d_M | ≈ 280–400 |
| t10 (unanticipated) | 3.33×10⁻² | 2.85×10⁻² | 4.8×10⁻³ | ≈ 1400 |
| t05 (unanticipated) | 8.61×10⁻³ | 8.17×10⁻³ | 4.4×10⁻⁴ | ≈ 15 000 |

The **dev results agree in order of magnitude** (pc2 random: median 171 episodes; t10: 1016; t05 mostly censored). The exact table is the justification for budget N = 3000 and for classing t05 as effectively undetectable within budget. The oracle LR test's cost ln(1/α)/d_M is a lower bound for any test, which the e-process meets up to the mixture offset and the margin loss.

**Power against specified alternatives:** pc0–pc2 (truth ∈ W): high. t05/t10: only via the margin. The experiment therefore measures *detectability of anticipated vs unanticipated inadequacy*, and any claim must carry that qualifier.

## 4. Class inadequacy versus distribution shift (item 7)
The test rejects "the stationary kernel on the probed class lies in M". It cannot tell a non-stationary truth from a stationary truth outside M; both falsify the stationary class. An exploratory control ("shift": E0 for the first third of the budget, then member n20) is included: it stays inside M at every moment, and the e-process is not built for it; dev rejection 0–2.5%. This shows only that a switch **between** members is rarely flagged; it is not a validity result. The report must say "class inadequacy" means "inadequacy of the *stationary* class".

## 5. Privileged information (item 8)
- The observer receives only its own actions and observations. The truth is built by the experimenter and accessed only through `Sampler.act`.
- **P4 and P5 use W**; P3 does not. P3's lack of W is a real information difference, which is why the fair design comparison for the *target variable* is P4 vs P5 (same information). The comparison P4 vs P2 (random) mixes information access and design target and is reported as such.
- **The positive controls are members of W**: the observer's alternative family contains the truth. Results on pc0–pc2 describe *anticipated* inadequacy and say nothing about unanticipated inadequacy.
- Experimenter-side exact computations (the choice of pc member by median KL) use the truth, but only to define the truth, never the observer.
- W is generated from E0 (the observer's own reference model) and a seed disjoint from the truth/run streams.

## 6. Other checks
- **Numerical stability**: all likelihoods in log space, probabilities clipped at 10⁻³⁰⁰; no non-finite values in 7200 dev runs.
- **Seeds**: dev 500–505; confirmatory 600–629 (disjoint). Between-seed SD of the log efficiency ratio in dev: 0.11–0.29 for P2/P4, up to 0.65 for P2/P3; with 30 seeds the 95% CI half-width on a log ratio is about ±0.11 (SD 0.3), enough to separate a ratio of 1.5 from ≲ 1.35 or ≳ 1.7. The dev point estimate for pc2 (P2/P4 = 1.42) lies close to 1.5, so the threshold is genuinely borderline for one control.
- **Reps within a seed share the POMDP instance**: CP bounds that pool runs assume independence and are therefore slightly optimistic; the seed-level bootstrap is used for efficiency ratios. Reported.

## 7. Gate decision
**D1: PASSED (self-review) for the revised design**, conditional on the preregistration being committed before confirmatory data. The Phase 6 proposal as written would not have been executed: it failed items 6 (power) and 8 (information confound).
