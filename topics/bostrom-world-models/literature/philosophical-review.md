### Read in full

**Chalmers (2024), "Taking the simulation hypothesis seriously", *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research*, DOI 10.1111/phpr.13122** (reply to commentators Godfrey-Smith, Schneider, Schwitzgebel; author's PDF). Relevant content: the simulation hypothesis cannot be ruled out and is not a sceptical hypothesis when the simulation is perfect, since most of our beliefs would be true. Per Chalmers's summary, Schneider and Schwitzgebel argue that *other, imperfect* versions may be sceptical; Chalmers himself writes that "a low-grade model would be pretty easily detectable" (in the context of how much simulation is needed for rich social interaction). (A search-tool summary also says he states that his arguments do not depend on simulations being undetectable; I did not find that sentence in the extracted text, so it is not relied on.) No statistical treatment of detectability appears in the passages read; I searched the extracted text for "detect", "glitch", "evidence", "imperfect", "indistinguish", "testab" and "empirical" and read all hits.

**Chalmers (2024), "The Simulation Hypothesis: Metaphysics, Epistemology, Value", in *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Mind* vol. 4, DOI 10.1093/9780198924159.003.0017 (author's PDF).** Three theses: if we are in a simulation the surrounding objects are real; we cannot know we are not in a simulation; we can live a good life in one. Epistemology: replying to Peacocke, he rejects the use of external perceptual evidence to rule out simulation (the "Sims won't have our evidence" appendix: with Weatherson 2003, externalists about evidence say a sim lacks our evidence; Chalmers argues that when most beings with experiences like ours are sims, this evidence is defeated, using zebra/hologram and 50%-cat analogies). The argument is by indifference over beings with matching experiences, not by statistical testing.

### Assessment

The qualitative content of theses T1 and T2 is **largely anticipated** by Chalmers's perfect/imperfect distinction and by the detection proposals in physics. The residue that may be new is (i) the explicit decomposition into three inferences with stated premises, (ii) quantitative bounds tied to a class of interventions (Props. 3–6), and (iii) the learned-world-model instantiation with the observer's own theory as part of the test. Whether any of these is new in the philosophical literature has not been established: the search was unsystematic, and several directly relevant sources (Brogioli, the glitch paper, Edge, Kelly's definitions, the Glymour–Malament–Manchak–Norton papers) were not read.


## Phase 4 update (2026-11-03)

### Sources read first-hand this phase

- **Bostrom (2003), full text.** Two points matter for this project. (1) The argument builds in indistinguishability: simulators can fill in detail on demand and "could easily edit the states of any brains that have become aware of an anomaly before it spoils the simulation", or rerun the simulation; the stated cost is of simulations "indistinguishable from physical reality for human minds". (2) The credence step is licensed by "a very weak indifference principle", stated for the case where "we don't have any information that indicate that our own particular experiences are any more or less likely than other human-type experiences to have been implemented in vivo rather than in machina". The argument is thus explicitly conditional on the absence of discriminating information.
- **Chalmers, "The Matrix as Metaphysics"** (via a fetch summary): the Matrix Hypothesis is metaphysical, not sceptical; "From the inside, there is no way to tell for sure that I am not in the situation of the brain in a vat"; Bostrom is cited.
- **Chalmers (2024)** two papers: read in full in Phase 3.

## Phase 5 update (2026-11-03)

### Record for each important work (thesis, assumptions, relation, reading status)

The detailed table with verified metadata, exact theses, and reading levels for the central works is in [novelty-threat-matrix.md](novelty-threat-matrix.md). Additional points from this phase:

- **Bostrom and Kulczycki (2011), full text.** The paper locates a mathematical non sequitur in the formula for the fraction of observers who are simulated and offers patches, one of which requires "empirical indexical evidence" with stated conditions; it also gives an example of what would be evidence against the simulation hypothesis (a defeater built on computer-age birth rank). *Relation to our claims:* it shows that Bostrom himself allows empirical evidence to bear on the hypothesis, but the evidence is **indexical** (where in history we are), not **detection** of artifacts from within; it does not discuss detectability by embedded observers. It does not anticipate the formal bound on discriminating evidence (Corollary), but it makes clear that the corollary concerns only one route by which evidence could bear on the argument.
- **SEP "Underdetermination" (summary).** States that "any judgment of empirical equivalence is both defeasible and relativized to a particular state of science" (Laudan and Leplin). *Relation:* anticipates the relativisation of equivalence to what is observable and to auxiliary assumptions; our intervention-class relativity (Proposition 1′) is an exact finite instance.
- **SEP "Abduction" (summary).** IBE as a reply to underdetermination and to brain-in-a-vat scepticism (simplicity and explanatory considerations favour the ordinary-world hypothesis); the "bad lot" objection; adding a catch-all hypothesis "gives little assurance, because that hypothesis is usually uninformative". *Relation:* the scientific-realism and IBE response is a philosophical route to rung 4 of our ladder that does **not** rely on observational detection (explanatory virtues set priors and likelihoods); our analysis is silent about it, and a manuscript must say so. The catch-all remark is the philosophical counterpart of Proposition 10(c).
- **Chalmers "The Matrix as Metaphysics" (summary).** The note in the Notre Dame review that Chalmers's conclusion strengthens the standard IBE reply to scepticism (because the matrix hypothesis is not sceptical) is relevant: on his view the question "are we in a simulation?" is not an epistemic threat, which changes the stakes of our results.

### Does the proposed philosophical thesis already exist?

Components: yes (see the threat matrix); the conjunction as an analysis of what a **fallible, learned observer** can infer, with a quantified account of Bostrom's proviso: not found in the sources reached, with the unread sources listed. The honest summary is **integration of established ideas with new controlled empirical instances**, not a new philosophical thesis.
