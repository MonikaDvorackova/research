## Summary

PROV-DM is the W3C conceptual data model for provenance: information about entities, activities, and agents involved in producing a piece of data or a thing, intended to support assessments of quality, reliability, or trustworthiness. It distinguishes core structures (entity, activity, generation, usage, communication, derivation, agent, attribution, association, delegation) from extended structures (bundles, specialization/alternate, collections, and further influence relations). Descriptions are organized in six components covering entities/activities and time, derivations, agents and responsibility, bundles (provenance of provenance), alternate/specialization links, and collections.

Without evaluation: PROV-DM provides a general graph vocabulary for “what was used/generated/derived,” “who was responsible,” optional plans associated with activities, optional timestamps, application-specific attributes, and named bundles of provenance statements. Extensibility is primarily via `prov:type`, `prov:role`, `prov:location`, and non-PROV-namespace attributes. Validity of composed descriptions is deferred to PROV-CONSTRAINTS.

## Strengths

1. **Normative, stable provenance vocabulary** for entities, activities, agents, usage, generation, and derivation — directly supports IC-23/IC-24 and much of “evidence used” / process structure.
2. **Responsibility model** (attribution, association, delegation) systematically addresses accountability-oriented human/organisational context at the provenance layer.
3. **Bundles** enable provenance of provenance and named sets of descriptions — useful integrity/meta-recording patterns when composed with storage controls.
4. **Specialization/alternate** provide principled hooks for version-like entity identity over time.
5. **Extensibility** is explicit (§6), so domain profiles *could* add decision-normative attributes — but that is profile work, not PROV-DM content.

---

## Limitations

1. PROV-DM defines provenance for production/derivation of things; it does **not** define justification, authorization, or validity of AI-supported *decisions* (aligned with claim-inventory **C5**, still a hypothesis pending broader kill-list results).
2. Optional plan on association is not a governing-policy or applicable-rules model.
3. No first-class relied-upon vs available evidence distinction.
4. Times and many relation fields are optional; historical faithfulness is not enforced by PROV-DM alone (validity constraints live in PROV-CONSTRAINTS, and still do not encode legal decision criteria).
5. Scoring here addresses **model expressiveness**. Real-world PROV deployments may retain even less; that is a separate empirical question.

---

## What this source does not establish

- That existing AI systems fail reconstruction requirements (checklist non-purpose; charter open question).
- That a new architectural layer is necessary (C8 remains open).
- That PROV profiles cannot encode the triad (counterargument in C5 remains live — would require an explicit profile evaluation).
- Completeness of the PROV family beyond PROV-DM (serializations, constraints, query).

---

## Uncertainties and scope limitations

- This review did not score PROV-CONSTRAINTS, PROV-O, or domain PROV profiles.
- Mapping “decision episode” onto Entity/Activity/Bundle is a modeling choice; different profiles could score IC-01 higher in a composition review.
- “Legally relevant” remains scenario-relative (checklist IC-20 conditional).

---

## Overall Assessment

**W3C PROV-DM substantially supports reconstruction of derivation, usage, activity structure, and agent responsibility.** It does **not**, by itself, systematically preserve the normative information required for faithful reconstruction of legally relevant AI-supported decisions under the repository checklist (governing policy and versions, applicable rules/constraints, relied-upon evidence, legal context).

**Disposition relative to C5:** This review supplies positive evidence that PROV-DM’s core relations answer lineage/responsibility questions more directly than justification/authorization/validity. It does **not** by itself promote C5 from `hypothesis` to `supported` across all provenance approaches — only for the PROV-DM unit as scoped.
