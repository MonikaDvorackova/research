## Title

A Knowledge Representation Framework for Historical AI Decision Reconstruction

## Abstract

Objective. We refine and characterize the historical decision reconstruction problem for AI systems as a knowledge-representation and AI-systems problem: recovering, as of decision time, the decision knowledge that identifies an AI-assisted Decision Episode and restates its justification, authorization, and temporal validity.

Methods. We distinguish execution artefacts from decision knowledge, derive technology-neutral reconstruction requirements (Reconstruction Checklist), perform unit evaluation of thirteen representative infrastructure sources, and conduct compositional evaluation via adversarial scenarios and binding analysis. We differentiate the approach from decision provenance and from the Decision Evidence Maturity Model (DEMM).

Findings. Reviewed infrastructure families are often capable for native purposes but do not jointly preserve sufficient decision knowledge under default composition without durable bindings to a shared Decision ID and decision-time versions (ecosystem base triad: FAIL). A derived Knowledge Continuity Model (KCM) instantiates the stated requirements under stated population and preservation assumptions (internal coverage: PASS under assumptions); this is not independent empirical confirmation.

Conclusions. The principal contribution is a refined representational and compositional characterization of historical decision reconstruction requirements across heterogeneous AI infrastructure. KCM and the conformance specification are derived engineering responses. Legal and governance domains motivate and stress-test the problem but are not the scientific object.

The characterization and compositional evaluation matter for knowledge representation, AI systems, long-lived agents, and explainability infrastructure: they separate logging/provenance/XAI mechanisms from Decision Episode reconstructability and identify binding requirements that default compositions do not guarantee.
