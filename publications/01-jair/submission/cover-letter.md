Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, *A Knowledge Representation Framework for Historical AI Decision Reconstruction*, for publication in JAIR as an original research article. The work has not been published elsewhere and is not under review at another venue.

The paper refines the historical decision reconstruction problem for artificial intelligence systems as a knowledge-representation and AI-systems question: whether retained structure supports recovery, as of decision time, of the decision knowledge that identifies an AI-assisted Decision Episode and restates its justification, authorization, and temporal validity. It distinguishes execution artefacts from decision knowledge, derives technology-neutral reconstruction requirements, and reports a unit and compositional evaluation of thirteen representative infrastructure families. From that analysis it derives an engineering response—the Knowledge Continuity Model and a conformance specification—presented as a consequence of the characterization rather than as an independently motivated headline architecture. Selected representational objects are further instantiated in a reference implementation (AIGov) to demonstrate implementation feasibility; that demonstration is not offered as independent validation of the theory.

The manuscript is intended for JAIR readers working on knowledge representation, AI systems, multi-agent and long-lived decision-support settings, and explainability infrastructure. Legal and governance regimes appear only as motivating or stress-test domains; they are not the scientific object of the paper. Related work on decision provenance and on the Decision Evidence Maturity Model (DEMM) is treated as complementary: provenance is a relevant substrate that can encode portions of a Decision Episode, and DEMM shares a reconstructability diagnosis while asking a different primary question about evidence sufficiency.
