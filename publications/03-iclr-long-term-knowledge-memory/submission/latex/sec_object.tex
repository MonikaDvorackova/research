\section{Cross-Query Consistency in Evolving Worlds}
\label{sec:object}

Let $W_0,\ldots,W_T$ be structured world states with events $E_{1:T}$ and $W_t=\mathrm{Apply}(W_{t-1},E_t)$. The gold history is retained for scoring only. The system receives observations $O_{1:T}$ and holds memory $M_T$. A dependent bundle $Q_b=\{q_1,\ldots,q_{n_b}\}$ asks interdependent current/historical/transition/relational/provenance/conflict questions about one focal lifecycle. Predictions $\hat Y_b=\{\hat y_i\}$ are produced by a reader on $M_T$. Constraints $\mathcal{C}_b$ are compiled from the gold history and query slots.

\paragraph{Operational definitions.}
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Acc}_i &= \mathbf{1}[\hat y_i \equiv y_i^*]
\quad\text{(structured exact match to gold)},
\label{eq:acci}\\
\Phi(\hat Y_b;\mathcal{C}_b) &= \mathbf{1}\bigl[\forall c\in\mathcal{C}_b:\; c(\hat Y_b)\bigr]
\quad\text{(joint world-history consistency)},
\label{eq:phi}\\
\Acc &= \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \mathrm{Acc}_i,\qquad
\BCR = \tfrac{1}{|B|}\sum_{b\in B}\Phi(\hat Y_b;\mathcal{C}_b),\qquad
\Gapq = \Acc-\BCR.
\label{eq:rates}
\end{align}
Here $N$ is the number of scored queries and $B$ the set of bundles. Acc$\in[0,1]$ and BCR$\in[0,1]$. If $\mathcal{C}_b=\emptyset$, we define $\Phi=1$ (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}). Acc aggregates query-local gold matches. $\Phi$ is a property of the entire answer set under world-history constraints---not mean Acc and not pairwise agreement alone (Figure~\ref{fig:inconsistency}).

\paragraph{Frozen vignette (B2 latest-only).}
Table~\ref{tab:b2vignette} shows one CEO-succession bundle from the frozen Option~A predictions. Six of eight items match gold ($\Acc=0.75$), including current CEO and the succession transition, but the system abstains on the historical CEO (and on temporal order). Under $\Phi=\mathbf{1}[\forall c\in\mathcal{C}_b:c(\hat Y_b)]$, those abstentions leave required slots unfilled, so transition/contradiction/temporal constraints fail and $\Phi=0$ for this belief set---hence a BCR contribution of $0$. Acc alone does not surface that joint failure. This is a metric-level illustration, not a claim that abstention always zeros BCR, nor a causal diagnosis of memory loss.

\paragraph{Proposition (identical Acc, different BCR).}
There exist prediction assignments with equal Acc but unequal BCR.
\emph{Minimal construction.} Two bundles of three gold-linked queries each; $N=6$. System~$A$: one fully gold bundle and one partial ($\Acc=4/6$, $\BCR=1/2$). System~$B$: two Acc hits per bundle but failed transitions ($\Acc=4/6$, $\BCR=0$). Empirically, frozen B2 and B3-$k3$ share Acc~$=$~0.4905 with BCR 0.219 vs.\ 0.000 (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{What $\Gapq$ means---and does not.}
A large positive $\Gapq$ means local gold matches occur often while fully coherent bundles are rarer. $\Gapq$ does \textbf{not} measure retrieval quality alone, factual correctness of a wrong-but-coherent world (\texttt{consistent\_wrong\_world}), overall memory usefulness, or real-world reliability.

\paragraph{Conjunction penalty (acknowledged).}
Because $\Phi$ requires $\forall c$, BCR is mathematically stricter as $|\mathcal{C}_b|$ and $n_b$ grow. We therefore report a diagnostic baseline $\widehat{\mathrm{AC}}_b=\Acc_b^{n_b}$ (i.i.d.\ item-correctness approximation) and compare BCR to both Acc and mean $\widehat{\mathrm{AC}}$. A gap $\Acc-\BCR$ that remains after comparing BCR to $\widehat{\mathrm{AC}}$ cannot be attributed solely to ``Acc to the power $n$.'' This baseline is \textbf{not} the true error process and does not replace primary BCR.
