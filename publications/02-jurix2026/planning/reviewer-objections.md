## Anticipated reviewer objections

Prepared responses for writing stage. Objections are hypothetical JURIX/AI & Law reviewer moves.

---

### Objection 1 — “Reviewability already covers this.”

**Likely source:** Cobbe, Lee & Singh (2021).

**Response strategy:** Agree that reviewability is the closest socio-technical neighbour. Differentiate: this paper isolates **Decision Knowledge** as an engineering artifact and **Knowledge Continuity** as a system property focused on preservation under technical change (prompts, retrieval, tools, models), for later legal reasoning. Cite Cobbe as foundation, not rival to erase.

---

### Objection 2 — “Provenance and audit logs are enough.”

**Response strategy:** Use §2 scenarios where logs/provenance exist but legal criteria, human rationale, prompt context, or policy version used at decision time are missing or unbound. Position PROV as substrate, not substitute (literature-map).

---

### Objection 3 — “Post-hoc XAI solves explainability.”

**Response strategy:** Invoke legal explainability literature (Wachter; Edwards & Veale) and central claim of §6: explanations cannot recover knowledge that was never preserved. XAI explains model behaviour; Decision Knowledge preserves decision-time context.

---

### Objection 4 — “No evaluation / not scientific.”

**Response strategy:** Declare contribution class: conceptual problem formulation + framework. Point to future work (formal modelling, cases, legal evaluation). Lean on §7 narrowness author note. Offer conceptual scenario if authors approve (open question).

---

### Objection 5 — “Terminology is buzzwordy.”

**Response strategy:** Use `terminology-review.md` recommendations; define early; disambiguate from KM “knowledge continuity” and from XAI outputs.

---

### Objection 6 — “This is an EU AI Act compliance note.”

**Response strategy:** Act is a stress-test domain, not the scientific object. Primary object: engineering preservation of decision knowledge for AI & Law functions. Calibrate Act depth (open question).

---

### Objection 7 — “Component list is arbitrary.”

**Response strategy:** Label as working inventory derived from legal functions in the RQ (explain, justify, review, contest). Invite refinement; optionally propose a minimal core vs extended set in discussion.

---

### Objection 8 — “Privacy and data minimisation forbid this.”

**Response strategy:** Treat as first-class limitation (§7): proportionality, retention limits, redaction, purpose limitation. Knowledge Continuity does not imply unbounded retention.

---

### Objection 9 — “Hidden commercial framework (AIGov).”

**Response strategy:** Do not centre product. If principles inspired by practice appear, describe them abstractly. Anonymise identifying project references for submission (spec requirement).

---

### Objection 10 — “How should’ RQ is too broad.”

**Response strategy:** Keep RQ; add operational sub-questions (`argument-map.md`). Answer with principles/framework, not a single algorithm.

---

### Objection 11 — “Insufficient engagement with AI & Law classics.”

**Response strategy:** Mandatory citations from JURIX/ICAIL/AI & Law journal on justification and explanation (Atkinson/Bench-Capon line; argumentation/CBR as needed). Harašta ownership.

---

### Objection 12 — “Contestable AI by design already lists the features.”

**Response strategy:** Alfrink et al. supply features/practices for contestation. This paper asks what **knowledge** those features consume later, and how systems must **preserve** it across change—complementary layer.
